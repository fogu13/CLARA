# CLARA Outreach Playbook

**For:** A solo founder in Berlin selling CLARA — an EU-sovereign, governed, self-improving Voice-of-Customer engine.
**Wedge:** EU data residency + GDPR / EU-AI-Act governance + industry-adaptable with no fine-tuning.
**Best-fit buyer:** HIGH-VOLUME public customer feedback (many app-store / Trustpilot / Google reviews, support complaints) **AND** a hard reason to keep data in the EU (regulated fintech/insurer, health data, incumbent bank, multi-vertical portal).
**Priority geography:** DACH (DE/AT/CH) first, then broader EU.

This playbook is the operating manual for turning the pooled prospect research into booked discovery calls — legally, cheaply, and as one person.

---

## 0. The one-page mental model

You are not "selling AI." You are selling **governed, EU-resident insight from feedback the prospect already has publicly, that they legally cannot pump into a US LLM.** Every message ties three things together:

1. **Their observable pain** (a specific, cited number — e.g. "35k Trustpilot reviews, 1-in-5 one-star").
2. **The residency/governance trap** (they can't legally send that corpus to ChatGPT/Claude-via-US).
3. **The CLARA payoff** (theme clustering + urgency/sentiment + auditable outcome loop, no fine-tuning, stays in the EU).

If a message doesn't contain all three, don't send it.

## 1. Finding the right decision-maker and their contact — GDPR-compliantly

### 1.1 Who to target (role map)

CLARA touches three buying centres. Approach in this order of receptiveness:

| Buying centre | Primary titles (search these) | Why they care |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer / CX** (warmest) | Head of Customer Experience, Chief Customer Officer, VP Customer Operations, Head of Customer Care/Service, Head of Customer Insights / VoC | They own the rating and the complaint backlog; feel the pain daily. |
| **Product** | VP Product, Head of Product Insights, Director of Product | They want structured themes to prioritise the roadmap. |
| **Risk / Compliance** (strongest for regulated fintech/insurer) | Chief Compliance Officer, Head of Risk, Data Protection Officer (DPO), Head of Complaints Management | For BaFin/FMA/FINMA-supervised firms the residency + auditability angle is a compliance win, not a nice-to-have. |

**Segment nuance:** in acute-regulator cases (Solaris, Postbank, Bitpanda under MiCA) lead with Compliance/Risk; in "app loved, service hated" cases (Scalable, Trade Republic, N26) lead with CX/Customer Ops; in multi-vertical portals (CHECK24, Verivox) target CX plus a per-vertical Ops/Quality head.

### 1.2 How to find the person (tooling for DACH)

- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator** (your primary weapon): filter by company + title keywords (`Kundenservice`, `Customer Experience`, `Beschwerde`, `Qualität`, `Compliance`, `Insights`, `Produkt`). German titles matter — search both EN and DE. Save leads into per-segment lists. This is where 80% of your first-touches originate.
- **Apollo.io**: enrich the person you found on LinkedIn; good for confirming role and getting a *business* email pattern. Treat emails as a research aid, not a green light to cold-email (see §1.3).
- **Dealfront (formerly Echobot + Leadfeeder)**: built for the DACH market and GDPR-compliant by design — best source for German company firmographics, Impressum data, trigger events, and website-visitor de-anonymisation once you have a landing page live.
- **Kaspr**: LinkedIn-native contact enrichment popular in EU; use to validate a direct line/mobile only when you have a legitimate reason to call (see UWG note on phone).
- **The Impressum / imprint page** (every prospect in the research has one, e.g. `n26.com/en-eu/imprint`, `check24.de/impressum`, `solarisgroup.com/en/imprint`): this is the *public, legitimate* company contact — general email, phone, registered address, and often the managing directors' names. Always start here; it's the GDPR-safe baseline.

### 1.3 The legal reality — read this before you email anyone

**German/DACH B2B cold email is restricted.** Under **UWG §7** (Gesetz gegen den unlauteren Wettbewerb), unsolicited advertising email requires **prior consent** or at least a **presumed/implied interest** (mutmaßliche Einwilligung) — a high bar the sender must justify. GDPR (Art. 6(1)(f) legitimate interest) governs *processing* the contact's data, but UWG governs whether you may *send the ad at all*, and it's stricter. A cold sales email to a random `firstname.lastname@company.de` is legally risky.

**Practical consequences for your sequencing:**

- **Safest first-touch channels (use these):**
  1. **LinkedIn** — connection request + message is *not* email advertising and is the de-facto accepted B2B channel in DACH.
  2. **Warm intro** — a mutual connection (investor, Berlin fintech/CX community, ex-colleague). Highest conversion, zero legal risk.
  3. **Events & communities** — Money20/20 Europe, Insurtech Insights Europe, FinanzBusiness/Handelsblatt events, CX-focused meetups, Berlin startup/founder circles, relevant Slack/LinkedIn groups. In-person or event-follow-up counts as warm.
  4. **Phone** — permitted B2B when there's presumed interest; use the **Impressum** general line or a validated direct line to reach the department, *not* a scraped private mobile.
- **Cold email — restricted, use last and carefully:** only to a clearly role-relevant business address (`kundenservice@`, or a person whose role is *obviously* interested), short, genuinely relevant to their public pain, with a working opt-out and full Impressum in your own footer. Even then, prefer to have engaged on LinkedIn first so the email is a follow-up to an existing touch, not a cold approach.
- **Never:** invent, guess, or scrape personal emails/phones; never buy a bulk German B2B list and blast it (classic UWG violation).

**Rule of thumb:** *LinkedIn first, email only after a reply or a warm signal.*

### 1.4 Your own compliance hygiene

- Keep a lightweight record of *why* each contact is a legitimate business interest (their public role + their company's public pain) — your Art. 6(1)(f) balancing note.
- Honour opt-outs instantly; keep a suppression list.
- Put a proper Impressum + privacy link in your email footer and on your site (you're a German founder — you need this anyway).

## 2. Outreach angle per segment

Same three-part message spine (§0), tuned per vertical.

### 2.1 Regulated fintech / neobanks / neobrokers (N26, Trade Republic, Scalable, Bitpanda, Vivid, Raisin, Solaris, Yuh)
- **Angle:** "You have tens of thousands of public reviews you legally can't feed to a US LLM. CLARA gives EU-sovereign, auditable theme synthesis of your account-freeze / transfer-stuck / support-unreachable complaints — and an outcome loop you can show BaFin/FMA/FINMA."
- **Lead value:** residency + auditability + regulator-evidence. Volume is your proof.
- **Hook data to cite:** the specific Trustpilot count and 1-star rate from the research (e.g. Trade Republic ~35k reviews / ~1-in-5 one-star; N26 ~475+ pages; Bitpanda ~15k; Scalable ~2.6/5).
- **Trigger-heavy sub-segment:** MiCA (crypto — Bitpanda), BaFin monitors (Solaris, Postbank) — lead with Compliance.

### 2.2 Insurance / insurtech / health insurer (CHECK24 insurance, Verivox, Getsafe, Clark, Ottonova, wefox)
- **Angle:** "Your reviews cluster around claims-denial, documents-delayed, and 'no response' themes. CLARA adapts to insurance language **with no fine-tuning**, tags urgency, and maps complaints to EU-AI-Act / conduct obligations — all EU-resident."
- **Lead value:** industry-adaptable (no fine-tuning) + special-category data safety (acute for Ottonova's health data → DPO is a real buyer).
- **Multi-vertical portals (CHECK24 350k+ reviews, Verivox 55k+):** angle on *separating themes per vertical* (insurance vs energy vs telco vs travel) and feeding each BU an action-ready insight — a job generic tools can't do.

### 2.3 Incumbent bank in crisis (Postbank / Deutsche Bank)
- **Angle:** "BaFin put a special representative on you over the migration backlog. CLARA gives governed, auditable, EU-sovereign theme tracking to *prove* you're clearing the worst-harm complaints — remediation evidence, not just a dashboard."
- **Lead value:** regulator-evidence at scale + deep pockets. Cite the BaFin notice and the +87% complaints figure.

### 2.4 (If reached) Mid-market B2B SaaS (Personio-type)
- **Angle:** "You process employee PII, so feedback analysis must stay EU-resident. CLARA clusters your G2/Capterra/Trustpilot + support tickets into governed themes (support-wait, contract/auto-renewal, pricing-notice) for Product and CS."
- **Lead value:** GDPR on employee data + existing VoC team that wants better inputs.

## 3. Trigger / timing signals to watch

Reach out *when something moves*. Set these up as monitors (Dealfront alerts, Google Alerts, LinkedIn "job changes" in Sales Navigator, Trustpilot page watching, app-store rating trackers, an RSS/monitor on regulator pages).

| Trigger | Why it's a buy-window | Where to watch |
|---|---|---|
| **Rating drop / review-volume spike** | Public pain just got worse and visible internally. | Trustpilot, App Store / Play rankings, review-monitor tools |
| **New CX / Product / Insights / Compliance hire** | New leader wants a quick win and a fresh tool budget in their first 90 days. | LinkedIn Sales Navigator job-change alerts |
| **Funding round** | Fresh budget + growth pressure + scaling-support pain. | Sifted, Finance Forward, TechCrunch, Dealfront |
| **Regulator action** (BaFin/FMA/FINMA notice, monitor, fine, MiCA/AML news) | Compliance urgently needs auditable evidence — your strongest wedge. | BaFin/FMA/FINMA sites, Finextra, Handelsblatt, PYMNTS |
| **App relaunch / IT migration / major outage** | Complaint surge + leadership scrutiny (Postbank archetype). | Company blog, status pages, press, downdetector-type signals |
| **Public complaint press / "silence" reviews trending** | Reputational pressure creates urgency (wefox, Bitpanda). | Trade press (Insurtech Digital, Finextra), Trustpilot themes |
| **New DPO appointed / GDPR enforcement news** | Data-residency becomes a board topic. | LinkedIn, DPA rulings |

**Timing discipline:** log which trigger justified each outreach — it's both your opener ("I saw…") and your Art. 6(1)(f) legitimacy note.

## 4. First-touch template approach per channel

Keep everything short, specific, non-generic, and referencing *their* public number. Templates are frames, not scripts — personalise the pain line every time.

### 4.1 LinkedIn connection request (no pitch, ≤300 chars)
> Hi {First name} — I work on EU-sovereign customer-feedback analysis for regulated {fintechs/insurers} and follow {Company}'s CX closely. Given the volume of public reviews you field, I'd value connecting. No pitch.

### 4.2 LinkedIn first message (after they accept)
> Thanks for connecting, {First name}. Quick observation, not a pitch: {Company} has ~{X} public Trustpilot reviews, with a recurring {theme, e.g. "account-freeze / support-unreachable"} cluster. Most teams can't legally run that corpus through US AI tools. I've built CLARA — an EU-resident engine that clusters those complaint themes with urgency/sentiment and an auditable outcome loop, no fine-tuning. Worth a 20-min look at what your top themes actually are? Happy to share a sample read of your public reviews.

### 4.3 Phone (Impressum line / validated department line)
Opening (say who, why now, one sentence of value, ask for the right person):
> "Guten Tag, mein Name ist {Name}, ich baue in Berlin CLARA — EU-sovereign feedback analysis. Ich rufe an, weil {Company} sehr viel öffentliches Kundenfeedback bekommt und ich zeigen kann, wie man daraus DSGVO-konform auswertbare Themen macht. Wer verantwortet bei Ihnen Customer Experience / Beschwerdemanagement?"

Goal of the call: get the right name + agreement to a follow-up, not to pitch cold.

### 4.4 Email (only after a warm signal / reply; role-relevant address)
- **Subject:** `{Company}: your ~{X} public reviews, EU-resident theme analysis`
- **Body (5 sentences max):** one line naming the trigger; one line naming the specific public theme cluster with a number; one line on the residency trap (can't legally use US LLMs on this); one line on the CLARA payoff (governed themes + urgency + auditable outcome loop, no fine-tuning, EU-resident); one clear ask ("15 minutes next week — I'll bring a sample read of your public reviews").
- **Footer:** full Impressum, privacy link, one-click opt-out.

### 4.5 Warm intro request (to a mutual connection)
> {Name}, you know {Person/Company} — I've built an EU-sovereign VoC engine that clusters their public complaint themes in an auditable, GDPR-safe way. Would a quick intro to their {Head of CX / Compliance} be reasonable? I'll make you look good — happy to send a 2-line forwardable.

### 4.6 The "sample read" offer (your best differentiator)
Because the reviews are public, you can **do the work first**: run CLARA on a slice of their public Trustpilot/app-store reviews and lead with the insight ("here are your top 5 complaint themes by volume and urgency, from your own public reviews"). This turns a cold pitch into a value-first, GDPR-clean demo — you're only processing public data, and it proves the product instantly.

## 5. Qualification questions (discovery call)

Use BANT-ish but tuned to CLARA's wedge. You're qualifying for **volume + residency-need + owner + trigger**.

**Pain & volume**
1. How many public reviews / support tickets do you handle a month, and across which channels (App Store, Play, Trustpilot, support inbox, survey)?
2. How do you currently turn that into themes — manual tagging, a tool, or not at all?
3. What are your top 3 complaint themes today, and how confident are you in that number?

**Residency & governance (the wedge)**
4. Are you allowed to send customer-feedback text to US-hosted AI tools, or does data need to stay in the EU?
5. Do you have GDPR / EU-AI-Act or regulator (BaFin/FMA/FINMA) requirements on how you process and audit this data?
6. Have you ever needed to *evidence* to a regulator that you're acting on complaint themes?

**Owner & process**
7. Who owns "we found this theme → we did something about it" — CX, Product, Compliance? (Confirm you're talking to the owner or can reach them.)
8. Is there an existing VoC/insights function, or is this greenfield?

**Trigger, budget, timing**
9. What changed recently — new leadership, a rating drop, a funding round, an audit — that puts this on your desk now?
10. Is there budget for a CX/insights tool this quarter, and what would you need to see to justify a pilot?

**Qualification bar:** strong prospect = ≥ thousands of public reviews **AND** a hard EU-residency/regulatory constraint **AND** an identifiable owner **AND** a live trigger. Two of four = nurture; one or none = deprioritise.

## 6. Weekly operating rhythm (solo founder)

- **Mon:** review trigger monitors (ratings, hires, funding, regulator news); update per-segment Sales Navigator lists.
- **Tue–Wed:** run "sample reads" on 3–5 triggered accounts; send LinkedIn connects + first messages.
- **Thu:** follow-ups, warm-intro asks, calls to Impressum lines for accounts that engaged.
- **Fri:** discovery calls + log everything (contact, trigger, legitimacy note, stage) in a simple CRM.
- **Cadence:** LinkedIn touch → wait 3–4 days → value message with sample read → wait → (only if engaged) email/call. Max ~3 touches before you park and re-trigger later.

---

## 7. Do / Don't summary

**Do:** lead with their public number; offer a value-first sample read on public data; start on LinkedIn/warm/events/phone; target CX first (Compliance for regulator-in-crisis accounts); log a legitimacy note per contact; honour opt-outs instantly.

**Don't:** cold-blast German business emails (UWG §7); scrape or guess personal emails/phones; buy bulk DACH lists; pitch before you've shown you understand their specific complaint themes; send a message missing any of pain / residency-trap / payoff.
