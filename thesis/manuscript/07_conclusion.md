# Chapter 7: Conclusion

Organisations record that an action was taken on feedback more readily than they establish whether the underlying problem improved (Forrester, 2025b; Homburg and Fürst, 2005). This thesis develops and examines CLARA, a governed workflow that links customer feedback to approved actions, recorded outcome measurements and reusable learning records. The evaluation establishes selected technical properties and bounded enrichment performance on one corpus. Whether these mechanisms improve practitioner decisions and customer outcomes remains to be established. Each research question is answered with its evidence status (§1.7; Appendix D).

RQ1 asked how a software artifact can operationalise the loop so that actions are governed, executed and measured for closure. The answer is an implemented, demonstrated architecture with regression-tested mechanisms: signals are enriched, themes become candidate actions, each approved action is bound to a versioned outcome contract, the window or follow-up checkpoint, on a fixed observation interval, decides target attainment with an explicit grade, and closed loops leave learning records whose retrieval weight decays with age (Chapter 4). Several of these mechanisms were implemented and tested but not deployed at the time of writing (§4.1; Appendix G.8). The refusing estimator has run on simulated outcome data only, and decay is an implemented design hypothesis whose benefit remains untested (§6.1). Whether the architecture improves decisions or resolves customer problems in practice is open.

RQ2 asked how such action-taking can be governed. The approval and provenance controls are specified and tested, and the regulatory interpretation is documented in Appendix B. The approval gate controls false positives (wrong or harmful actions reaching an external system); it cannot recover escalation-worthy signals that the enrichment ranks too low, 22 of 50 under the seed labels on the production rubric. Whether people understand the controls, catch errors and accept their cost remains open: the practitioner study was not conducted.

RQ3a asked how closely the enrichment pipeline agrees with reference labels. The status is measured (bounded). On 188 English paraphrases of public reviews, the production configurations reached sentiment agreement of 0.83–0.84 with star-rating proxies on 153 items, against 0.78 for TF-IDF + logistic regression, a difference not distinguishable on this corpus after the retrospective Holm correction; the generic prompt on GLM-5.2 reached 0.86, a lead that is stable across repeated runs on the same items and not significant after the correction; against assistant-authored risk labels on 106 items, production agreement was 0.50–0.59 (§5A). Neither gold standard is human-labelled: the star ratings are a human-origin proxy and the remaining seeds are assistant judgements (§3.7). Agreement with independent human judgement and performance on natural deployment data are open.

RQ3b asked whether the choice of triage method changes whose problems are escalated. The evidence is descriptive: escalation recall differs across source strata, but the German-source positive stratum has eight cases, language is confounded with sector, and the texts are English paraphrases, so nothing here measures German-text processing (§5A.5). No language effect, fairness ranking of methods or evidence of parity is claimed; per-stratum monitoring is the design consequence (§6.5).

RQ4 remains unanswered because the practitioner study was not conducted. The current evidence supports the feasibility of the architecture and bounded agreement with reference labels, but does not establish practitioner usefulness or improved customer outcomes. The protocol, instruments and pre-registered reporting tiers are retained (Appendix A; §5B) so that sessions run before submission can be reported.

Within the literature and product documentation reviewed in Chapters 2 and 6 (§2.7; §6.3), no evaluated system was identified that combines feedback enrichment, governed action under human approval, contracted and graded outcome measurement, and perishable, retrievable learning records in one artifact. The contribution is therefore the integrated design, the provisional design principles of Chapter 6, and evidence about the limits of the enrichment and measurement components. The limits of these claims are stated in §6.4. The agenda of §6.5 begins with the practitioner study and the human annotation study; until that work is done, the claims of this thesis remain bounded.
