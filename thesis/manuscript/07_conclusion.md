# Chapter 7: Conclusion

This thesis set out to address a well-documented but stubborn problem: organisations collect abundant customer feedback, yet closing the loop on it remains the weakest practice in feedback management, and the communication of feedback to those who could act is too slow to drive action (Forrester, 2025b). The binding constraint is not collection or even analysis but **operationalisation under governance**: turning insight into action that is routed, executed, *measured*, and *remembered*. Framed as Design Science Research, the work designed, built, and evaluated a working platform that instantiates the full **Signal → Insight → Action → Learning** cycle, with Responsible-AI controls (human-in-the-loop approval, audit trails, transparency, and EU AI Act / GDPR alignment) embedded as properties of the loop rather than as a bolt-on.

The contributions answer the research questions in the order in which the evidence can carry them. What is **measured** answers RQ3a and RQ3b. A reproducible evaluation on **188 real customer signals from English- and German-language sources** (scored as English paraphrases; §3.7) showed that triage accuracy is strongly method-dependent: a rule-based lexicon floor is inadequate on paraphrased operational feedback (sentiment accuracy 0.37, risk macro-F1 0.26), a lightweight learned model reaches usable accuracy (0.78, 0.65), and a contextual LLM path on the artifact's model is the strongest predictor on sentiment (0.86, a paired lead over the learned model that holds in four runs, McNemar p = 0.019–0.029 unadjusted and 0.087 after Holm correction, so replicated but exploratory) and level with the learned model on risk (macro-F1 0.68 in the reported run, 0.60–0.61 in three repeats). The artifact's own enrichment stage, run on the same model and on the EU-hosted production default, scores within three points of that prompt on sentiment (0.83 and 0.84) and one urgency level below the seed labels on risk (0.50 and 0.59: the production rubric calls "high" what the seed labels call "critical"), reported as a measured, uneven capability rather than adjusted after the fact (§5A.4.2). The loop's precondition is therefore satisfiable by more than one method, and the choice among them is a design trade-off. On escalation equity, the eight German-source signals whose reference label warrants escalation are escalated by the learned model at 62.5% against 95.1% for the 41 English-source ones, by the contextual path at 87.5% against 87.8% (75% against 78–81% in three repeats), and by the keyword floor not at all; these are descriptive stratum figures that rank no predictor (Gelman & Stern, 2006), and the answer to **RQ3b** is indicative: method choice can change whose problems reach a human, so escalation has to be monitored per stratum in deployment. A second, in-repo gold standard, authored with an AI coding assistant and no more human-labelled than the seed labels (§3.7), supplies convergent evidence: 99% sentiment and 91% urgency on the artifact's 100-case golden set in the 6 September 2026 snapshot, 96.7% and 93.3% on a held-out half that was consulted on every run, and an in-run few-shot lift that is significant on tag vocabulary on both models and on urgency on GLM-5.2 only, held as exploratory. That the production stage scores 0.83 against the independent star proxy and 0.97–0.99 against curated labels, on the same model and pipeline, is itself instructive: what differs is the gold standard and the label scheme, and the conservative figure is the one this thesis quotes. What is **demonstrated** answers RQ1 and RQ2: actions are bound to outcome contracts and measured for recurrence with a graded, refusing estimator, learnings are retained as perishable, retrievable memory, every action is a draft whose approval is graduated by consequence class and recorded against a hashed evidence pack, and the alignment with the EU AI Act and GDPR is set out as a design walkthrough that says which articles bind and which are adopted voluntarily (Appendix B); these commitments are abstracted into five transferable design principles and two implementation decisions (Chapter 6). What remains **exploratory** is RQ4: the practitioner study's protocol, instruments and fallback reporting rule are in place, and its results are reported at whatever sample it reaches, labelled as such (§5B).

The work also clarifies the artifact's position in a crowded market. Where leading feedback-intelligence platforms detect and route, this artifact's defensible distinction is **measured closure plus a decaying learning memory under explicit governance**, integrated with rather than competing against the tools organisations already use.

Its limitations are stated plainly. The quantitative corpus is modest and balanced rather than population-representative. Four of its six gold-labelled fields are scored, and two of those, journey stage and recommended owner, only under a supplied closed inventory, which makes their figures an upper bound on free-form production routing, while theme and recommended action carry labels but no metric at all. Language is confounded with sector in the corpus, and the cross-language claim is confined to escalation recall on a German-source stratum of eight signals. The qualitative study is small-N and establishes design validity, not causal effect. And the researcher is also the designer. These bound the claims to transferable design knowledge and a situated instantiation, which is exactly what Design Science Research offers, rather than to universal or commercial certainty.

The broader contribution is a demonstration that an action-taking AI system in customer operations can be **both useful and responsible by design**: closing the loop and governing the closing are not in opposition, but two halves of the same well-designed workflow. The same verify-before-believe standard the artifact imposes on its own actions was applied to the thesis's own claims, which is the only basis on which a researcher who is also the builder can ask to be believed; Appendix D records where that standard left each claim. Turning a measured loop into demonstrated, longitudinal business value, and extending the governance from design commitment to continuous, monitored practice, is the work that remains.
