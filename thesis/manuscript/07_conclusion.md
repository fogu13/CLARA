# Chapter 7: Conclusion

This thesis set out to address a well-documented but stubborn problem: organisations collect abundant customer feedback yet fewer than a third systematically close the loop on it (Bone et al., 2017). The binding constraint is not collection or even analysis but **operationalisation under governance** — turning insight into action that is routed, executed, *measured*, and *remembered*. Framed as Design Science Research, the work designed, built, and evaluated a working platform that instantiates the full **Signal → Insight → Action → Learning** cycle, with Responsible-AI controls — human-in-the-loop approval, audit trails, transparency, and EU AI Act / GDPR alignment — embedded as properties of the loop rather than as a bolt-on.

The contributions answer the research questions as follows. For **RQ1** and **RQ2**, the artifact demonstrates that the loop can be closed by design: actions are bound to outcome contracts and verified for resolution, learnings are retained as perishable, retrievable memory, and autonomy is graduated and auditable — abstracted into six transferable design principles (Chapter 6). For **RQ3**, a reproducible evaluation on **188 real, multilingual customer signals** showed that triage accuracy is strongly method-dependent: a rule-based lexicon floor is inadequate on paraphrased operational feedback (sentiment accuracy 0.37, risk macro-F1 0.26), while a lightweight learned model reaches usable accuracy (sentiment 0.78, risk macro-F1 0.65), with the artifact's contextual LLM path wired for a further, pending lift. This is a substantive, honest result — naive triage fails on real feedback, and learned models are necessary. For **RQ4**, a complete qualitative study — protocol, instruments, and analysis plan — is in place to assess practitioner usefulness, usability, and trust, with results to follow from the interview data.

The work also clarifies the artifact's position in a crowded market: where leading feedback-intelligence platforms detect and route, this artifact's defensible distinction is **measured closure plus a decaying learning memory under explicit governance**, integrated with rather than competing against the tools organisations already use.

Its limitations are stated plainly. The quantitative corpus is modest and balanced rather than population-representative; the strongest predictor's corpus run is pending; the qualitative study is small-N and establishes design validity, not causal effect; and the researcher is also the designer. These bound the claims to transferable design knowledge and a situated instantiation — exactly what Design Science Research offers — rather than to universal or commercial certainty.

The broader contribution is a demonstration that an action-taking AI system in customer operations can be **both useful and responsible by design**: that closing the loop and governing the closing are not in opposition, but are two halves of the same well-designed workflow. Turning a measured loop into demonstrated, longitudinal business value — and extending the governance from design commitment to continuous, monitored practice — is the work that remains.
