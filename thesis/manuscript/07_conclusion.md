# Chapter 7: Conclusion

This thesis set out to address a well-documented but stubborn problem: organisations collect abundant customer feedback yet fewer than a third systematically close the loop on it (Bone et al., 2017). The binding constraint is not collection or even analysis but **operationalisation under governance** — turning insight into action that is routed, executed, *measured*, and *remembered*. Framed as Design Science Research, the work designed, built, and evaluated a working platform that instantiates the full **Signal → Insight → Action → Learning** cycle, with Responsible-AI controls — human-in-the-loop approval, audit trails, transparency, and EU AI Act / GDPR alignment — embedded as properties of the loop rather than as a bolt-on.

The contributions answer the research questions as follows. For **RQ1** and **RQ2**, the artifact demonstrates that the loop can be closed by design: actions are bound to outcome contracts and verified for resolution, learnings are retained as perishable, retrievable memory, and autonomy is graduated and auditable — abstracted into six transferable design principles (Chapter 6). For **RQ3**, a reproducible evaluation on **188 real, multilingual customer signals** showed that triage accuracy is strongly method-dependent: a rule-based lexicon floor is inadequate on paraphrased operational feedback (sentiment accuracy 0.37, risk macro-F1 0.26), a lightweight learned model reaches usable accuracy (sentiment 0.78, risk macro-F1 0.65), and the artifact's contextual LLM path is the strongest predictor on both tasks (sentiment 0.86, risk 0.72). That the contextual gain is large on sentiment and slim on severity is itself part of the answer: the loop's precondition is satisfiable by more than one method, and the choice among them is a design trade-off rather than a foregone conclusion. The sharpest consequence is not accuracy but escalation equity — the keyword floor escalates none of the eight genuinely critical German signals while catching a fifth of the English ones, whereas the contextual path reaches near-parity (87.5% against 87.8%) — so method choice determines whose problems reach a human, on a corpus where language is confounded with sector and the German stratum is small. A second, independent gold standard supplies convergent evidence: on the artifact's own committed bilingual golden set, it reaches 97.5% sentiment and 86.3% urgency accuracy under the production configuration, with a statistically significant few-shot lift on exact tag-vocabulary agreement (McNemar p = 0.001) and its limits — the authored German stratum, the English-scope hallucination check, the still-underpowered urgency comparison — disclosed in the model card. This is a substantive, honest result — naive triage fails on real feedback, learned models are necessary, and the strongest method clears the accuracy bar the loop requires. For **RQ4**, a complete qualitative study — protocol, instruments, and analysis plan — is in place to assess practitioner usefulness, usability, and trust, with results to follow from the interview data.

The work also clarifies the artifact's position in a crowded market: where leading feedback-intelligence platforms detect and route, this artifact's defensible distinction is **measured closure plus a decaying learning memory under explicit governance**, integrated with rather than competing against the tools organisations already use.

Its limitations are stated plainly. The quantitative corpus is modest and balanced rather than population-representative; the strongest predictor's corpus run is pending; the qualitative study is small-N and establishes design validity, not causal effect; and the researcher is also the designer. These bound the claims to transferable design knowledge and a situated instantiation — exactly what Design Science Research offers — rather than to universal or commercial certainty.

The broader contribution is a demonstration that an action-taking AI system in customer operations can be **both useful and responsible by design**: that closing the loop and governing the closing are not in opposition, but are two halves of the same well-designed workflow. An unplanned external-review episode late in the work (§5A.8) put that claim under adversarial pressure and left it stronger: two independent LLM-based strategic reviews were verified claim by claim rather than believed, answered with twenty governance-hardening pull requests, and confirmed in production by seventeen live checks — the same verify-before-believe discipline the artifact imposes on its own actions, applied to the artifact itself. Turning a measured loop into demonstrated, longitudinal business value — and extending the governance from design commitment to continuous, monitored practice — is the work that remains.
