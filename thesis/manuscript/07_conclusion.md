# Chapter 7: Conclusion

This thesis set out to address a well-documented but stubborn problem: organisations collect abundant customer feedback, yet closing the loop on it remains the weakest practice in feedback management, and the communication of feedback to those who could act is too slow to drive action (Forrester, 2025b). The binding constraint is not collection or even analysis but **operationalisation under governance**: turning insight into action that is routed, executed, *measured*, and *remembered*. Framed as Design Science Research, the work designed, built, and evaluated a working platform that instantiates the full **Signal → Insight → Action → Learning** cycle, with Responsible-AI controls (human-in-the-loop approval, audit trails, transparency, and EU AI Act / GDPR alignment) embedded as properties of the loop rather than as a bolt-on.

The contributions answer the research questions in the order in which the evidence can carry them. What is **measured** answers RQ3a and RQ3b. A reproducible evaluation on **188 real customer signals from English- and German-language sources** (scored as English paraphrases; §3.7) showed that triage accuracy is strongly method-dependent. A rule-based lexicon floor is inadequate on paraphrased operational feedback (sentiment accuracy 0.37, risk macro-F1 0.26), a lightweight learned model reaches usable accuracy (sentiment 0.78, risk macro-F1 0.65), and a contextual LLM path on the artifact's model is the strongest predictor on sentiment and level with the learned model on risk (sentiment 0.86, risk macro-F1 0.68 in the reported run; the paired sentiment lead over the learned model is statistically significant in each of four runs, McNemar p = 0.019–0.029, though a single flipped item would put the reported run at 0.043 or 0.052, while on risk there is no reliable lead: three repeats score 0.60–0.61 macro-F1 against the learned model's 0.65, p ≥ 0.64 throughout). The artifact's own enrichment stage, run on the same corpus on the same model and on the EU-hosted production default, scores within three points of that prompt on sentiment (0.83 and 0.84; neither difference separable from the prompt, and its lead over the learned model not significant at this n) and one urgency level below the seed labels on risk (0.50 and 0.59: the production rubric calls "high" what the seed labels call "critical"), which the thesis reports as a measured, uneven capability rather than adjusting the rule after the fact (§5A.4.2). That the contextual gain is large on sentiment and slim on severity is itself part of the answer: the loop's precondition is satisfiable by more than one method, and the choice among them is a design trade-off rather than a foregone conclusion. The sharpest consequence is not accuracy but escalation equity. The learned model escalates 62.5% of the genuinely critical German-source signals against 95.1% of the English-source ones, a gap that is statistically significant (Fisher p = 0.026), while the contextual path's difference between the strata is not detectable (87.5% against 87.8%, p = 1.0; 75% against 78–81% in three repeats); the keyword floor escalates none of the eight German-source signals, but eight signals cannot separate that from chance. Method choice can determine whose problems reach a human. That is the answer to **RQ3b**, and it is indicative rather than established: the corpus confounds language with sector, the German-source gold-escalate stratum is eight signals, the strata are source-language strata of English paraphrases rather than German text, and a learned model trained on this corpus inherits its imbalance in a way a zero-shot model does not. A second, in-repo gold standard, authored in the repository with an AI coding assistant and so no more human-labelled than the seed labels (§3.7), supplies convergent evidence: on the artifact's own committed 100-case bilingual golden set, the production enrichment stage on GLM-5.2 reaches 99% sentiment and 91% urgency accuracy in the 6 September 2026 snapshot (96.7% and 93.3% on the held-out half, n = 30), with a statistically significant few-shot lift on exact tag-vocabulary agreement (McNemar p = 0.002; p = 0.001 in July) and on urgency (78% → 91%, p < 0.001; 83% → 90%, p = 0.039 in July), a claim withheld at n = 80 and held as exploratory until the held-out read; on the production default the tag lift replicates (p = 0.001) and the urgency lift does not (82% → 82%). That the production stage scores 0.83 against the independent star proxy and 0.97–0.99 against curated labels, on the same model and the same pipeline, is itself instructive: what differs is the gold standard and the label scheme, and the conservative figure is the one this thesis quotes. Naive triage fails on real feedback, learned models are necessary, and the strongest method clears the accuracy bar the loop requires. What is **demonstrated** answers RQ1 and RQ2. The artifact shows that the loop can be closed by design: actions are bound to outcome contracts and measured for recurrence with a graded, refusing estimator, learnings are retained as perishable, retrievable memory, and every action is a draft whose approval is graduated by consequence class and recorded against a hashed evidence pack; the alignment with the EU AI Act and GDPR is set out as a design walkthrough that says which articles bind and which are adopted voluntarily (Appendix B). These commitments are abstracted into five transferable design principles and two implementation decisions (Chapter 6). What remains **exploratory** is RQ4: the practitioner study's protocol, instruments and fallback reporting rule are in place, and its results are reported at whatever sample it reaches, labelled as such (§5B).

The work also clarifies the artifact's position in a crowded market. Where leading feedback-intelligence platforms detect and route, this artifact's defensible distinction is **measured closure plus a decaying learning memory under explicit governance**, integrated with rather than competing against the tools organisations already use.

Its limitations are stated plainly. The quantitative corpus is modest and balanced rather than population-representative. Four of its six gold-labelled fields are scored, and two of those, journey stage and recommended owner, only under a supplied closed inventory, which makes their figures an upper bound on free-form production routing, while theme and recommended action carry labels but no metric at all. Language is confounded with sector in the corpus, the texts are English paraphrases of multilingual sources, and the cross-language claim is confined to escalation recall on a German-source stratum of eight signals. The qualitative study is small-N and establishes design validity, not causal effect. And the researcher is also the designer. These bound the claims to transferable design knowledge and a situated instantiation, which is exactly what Design Science Research offers, rather than to universal or commercial certainty.

The broader contribution is a demonstration that an action-taking AI system in customer operations can be **both useful and responsible by design**: closing the loop and governing the closing are not in opposition, but two halves of the same well-designed workflow. The same verify-before-believe standard the artifact imposes on its own actions was applied to the thesis's own claims, which is the only basis on which a researcher who is also the builder can ask to be believed; Appendix D records where that standard left each claim. Turning a measured loop into demonstrated, longitudinal business value, and extending the governance from design commitment to continuous, monitored practice, is the work that remains.
