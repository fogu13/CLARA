# Chapter 6: Discussion

This chapter synthesises the design and evaluation into transferable knowledge. It abstracts the artifact's design decisions into **design principles** (RQ1, RQ2), reflects on the **Responsible-AI tensions** the work surfaced, positions the artifact against the **competitive landscape**, and states the **limitations** and **future work**. Consistent with the design-science stance (Gregor & Hevner, 2013), the contribution is offered as nascent design theory, prescriptive knowledge that should transfer to other feedback-to-action systems, not as a universal law.

## 6.1 Design Principles

The design decisions of Chapter 4 (§4.3, §4.4) and the architectural stance (§4.2) generalise into **five design principles** for governed feedback-to-action systems and **two implementation decisions**. The labels DP1–DP6 are kept from the proposal for traceability (Appendix D), but they are not of one kind: DP3 and DP6 are competent engineering choices an examiner may fairly call implementation rather than contribution, and they are demoted to that status below; DP7 is new, abstracted from the design cycle in §4.4 and §6.2 that measured a capability and declined to ship it. Each principle is stated with its **cost**, because a design principle that buys something for nothing is usually a platitude.

**DP1: Make closure a contracted, graded measurement that refuses when it is underpowered.** Bind every action, at approval, to an explicit outcome metric, baseline, window and success threshold; measure it on a schedule from raw signals; grade every readout by the design that produced it (A–E) and re-grade it by the fit actually obtained; and refuse to report an interval when the series cannot carry one, reporting a labelled delta instead (§3.5.5, §4.4). This is what lets the system claim a loop *closed* rather than merely *acted*, the distinction the practitioner evidence says is most often missing (Forrester, 2025c), and what stops a weak measurement from wearing the clothes of a strong one. The field evidence that recovery actions can have null or *negative* effects (Halperin et al., 2022) makes this principle load-bearing rather than decorative: without measurement, a feedback-to-action system can industrialise harm as efficiently as help. *Cost:* it forces teams to commit to a success metric up front, exactly the discipline most lack; a closure metric can be gamed by choosing an easy one, so the contract governs only as well as the metric is chosen; and a measurement that refuses is a measurement that often says nothing, since at the signal rates a mid-sized organisation produces the estimator's own break-even is tens of signals a day (§3.5.5).

**DP2: Treat organisational memory as perishable.** Model learnings as decaying and inspectable (confidence decay over a half-life, with staleness flags), so past results inform but do not ossify present decisions (Argote, 2013; Walsh & Ungson, 1991; for the prior art in LLM decay-memory and the precise novelty boundary, see §4.3.2 and Zhong et al., 2024). This principle has moved from argued to *partially measured*. In the artifact's instrumented evaluation, retrieving stored learnings raised the share of a past remedy appearing in new recommendations from 21% to 71% (§5A.7), so the memory demonstrably steers recommendations. The outcomes behind those learnings remain simulated, so *whether the steered recommendation is better* is still open. *Cost:* the half-life is a parameter the organisation rarely knows. Set it wrong and the system discards knowledge that still holds, or trusts knowledge that has died. Decay encodes *plausible* forgetting, not *correct* forgetting.

**DP3 (implementation decision): Let severity emerge from the set, not the loudest member.** Compute priority by fusing the corroborating set (strongest urgency, volume, negative proportion) rather than any single signal, by analogy with data-fusion conflict resolution (Bleiholder & Naumann, 2008). This mitigates both single-loud-complaint over-escalation and the dilution of widespread, low-urgency problems. *Cost:* fusion can bury the rare, catastrophic single signal, the lone fraud report or safety defect, that volume-weighting is structurally inclined to under-rank. The rule needs an explicit override for low-frequency, high-severity cases.

**DP4: Graduate authority by consequence class; every action is a draft.** Let the system propose, rank and route, and let a person decide: no action reaches an external system without a recorded approval, and what varies with the consequence of an action is the depth of review (one approver, two for consequential actions, none possible while a blocking policy check fails), not whether review happens (Parasuraman et al., 2000; §4.3.1). The first design cycle resolved this differently, with per-rule `auto_execute` flags and an ordered conflict resolver (§4.7); the deployed build retired that because the pipeline's routing accuracy (§5A.4.1) is that of a draft, and a rule set's transparency pays off only if someone maintains it, whereas an approval record is produced by the act of deciding. *Cost:* throughput is bounded by reviewer attention, and a gate everyone must pass invites rubber-stamping (§6.2), so the principle only holds together with attributable approvals and monitoring of how approvals are actually made.

**DP5: Keep the model bounded and the loop deterministic around it.** Confine language-model reasoning to named stages behind a single interface, with deterministic code for validation, conflict resolution, execution, and audit (§4.2). The §5A finding that triage accuracy is strongly method-dependent reinforces the point: the model is a component to be contained, not a controller to be trusted. *Cost:* bounding the model caps its ceiling. You forgo the open-ended flexibility of a free-roaming agent, and you must build and maintain the deterministic scaffolding yourself, trading adaptivity for governability and testability.

**DP6 (implementation decision): Adapt by configuration, not retraining.** Where triage priorities genuinely differ by context (sector, market), encode the difference as inspectable configuration, named weight profiles over a fixed scoring model (§4.3.5), rather than as per-context fine-tuned models. The cross-industry evaluation supports the premise: the same pipeline produced sector-appropriate vocabularies on fintech, food-delivery, and B2B-manufacturing feedback with no per-sector training (§5A.7). *Cost:* profiles are authored priors. They can encode the designer's sector stereotypes as easily as sector expertise, and every profile is configuration to maintain. Inspectability is the safeguard, not a guarantee.

**DP7: Where a capability's errors fall unevenly on the people the system serves, measure it, publish the measurement, and do not ship it.** The AI-authorship detector of §4.4 is the instance: two plausible detectors were built and measured on 6,740 real texts, both failed in the direction that matters (flagging genuine, articulate, angry complaints, unevenly across languages), and the per-comment capability was not shipped while the measurements were published and the gate was made to abstain explicitly where text length cannot support a judgement. The same rule governs the ITS estimator's refusal to fit and the routing fields' status as drafts (§5A.4.1). It is the operational form of the fairness stance in §2.12: a governed system's obligation is not only to be accurate on average but to know, and say, on whom it is wrong. *Cost:* it is commercially uncomfortable, since a competitor will ship the capability and market it; and it needs the measurement infrastructure (a labelled corpus, per-stratum reporting) to exist before the product decision can be made honestly.

**Why not an agent.** A reasonable objection to the whole design is that a capable language-model agent could simply route the problem, open the ticket and follow up, and that the approval interrupt is friction dressed as governance. The evaluation supplies the empirical answer: under the easiest condition, with the workspace's own inventory supplied, the pipeline assigns the right owner two times in five and the right journey stage three times in five (§5A.4.1). An agent acting on those judgements unattended would misroute most problems quietly. The industrial precedent points the same way: the HULA agent deployed inside Jira reduced engineers' effort when it was *steered* rather than left to act (Takerngsaksiri et al., 2024), and Gartner (2025) expects more than 40% of agentic-AI projects to be cancelled by 2027, citing escalating costs, unclear business value and inadequate risk controls, the last two being the failures a draft-and-approve loop is built to avoid. The bounded design (DP4, DP5) is therefore not a concession to regulation but the configuration the accuracy evidence supports; the day the routing numbers justify unattended action for some consequence class, DP4's graduation is the place to express it, and the approval records are the data that would justify it.

## 6.2 Responsible-AI Reflections and Tensions

Treating Responsible AI as an integral design dimension (rather than a compliance layer) surfaced genuine tensions worth naming.

**Oversight versus throughput.** EU AI Act Article 14-style human approval is the artifact's central safeguard, but every approval gate is friction. The design response, requiring an approval for every action and graduating only the depth of review by consequence class (§4.3.1), is itself a value judgement about which actions are consequential enough for two approvers, and the qualitative study (H6, H7) is designed to learn whether practitioners find that boundary trustworthy, or merely slow. There is no neutral default here. More oversight is safer and slower, and the *right* setting is contextual. The deeper tension is that oversight can be *nominal*: legal analysis of Article 14 argues that its awareness-based measures are unlikely to de-bias human overseers given the automation-bias evidence (Laux & Ruschemeier, 2025). An approval gate therefore earns its safety claim only through design (friction proportional to risk, visible evidence and a plainly-labelled model score, and per-decision attributability; Siebert et al., 2023) and through monitoring for rubber-stamping (§4.9), not through the gate's mere existence. The July 2026 revision hardened the gate in that spirit. The approving reviewer's identity is now bound to the verified login (a JWT-derived pseudonym) rather than self-asserted in the request body, where previously any editor could sign as someone else. An opt-in **four-eyes** mode requires two distinct approvers for consequential actions and rejects a reviewer confirming their own approval. And the German works-council pack carries a code-verifiable *no-individual-performance-monitoring* attestation backed by a CI test that calls every read endpoint as a non-admin and fails if any person-capable field leaks: the oversight claim held as continuously executed evidence rather than documentation, with its residual limits (the designated admin role still sees identities; admin-access logging is not yet built) stated in the attestation itself.

**Auditability versus simplicity.** A full audit trail (Article 12; GDPR Article 22) is what makes automated action accountable, but it adds interface and cognitive load. The design bet is that visibility of *why* an action fired (which rule, which signal, which outcome) increases appropriate trust (Lee & See, 2004); the TAM trust items test that bet. The July 2026 revision extended the bet from explanation to **honesty as a feature**. The UI's "confidence" label was renamed **model score** and explained as an uncalibrated heuristic rather than a validated probability. Every outcome readout now carries an **evidence grade** (A–E) that grades the measurement *design*, from randomised holdout down to manual assertion. Each measurement discloses its **provenance** (instrumented versus "unverified manual observation", stamped server-side so clients cannot spoof it). Guardrails without a data source surface an explicit "no data source" marker instead of silence, and near-duplicate signals are annotated, never dropped. Each of these adds interface load, so the tension stands, but they shift the trust question from *whether the user believes the number* to *whether the user knows what kind of number it is*.

**Accuracy, fairness, and language.** The evaluation made a fairness concern concrete, and not only for the deliberately weak floor: the learned model escalates critical German-source signals at 62.5% against 95.1% for English-source ones, a stratum gap that is statistically significant (Fisher exact p = 0.026), while the contextual path's stratum difference is not detectable on this corpus (87.5% against 87.8%, p = 1.0; §5A.5). One rival explanation is named rather than dismissed: the learned model is trained by cross-validation on this very corpus, in which German-source escalation cases are eight of forty-nine and almost all B2B vocabulary, so any in-domain classifier inherits that composition, and the zero-shot contextual model does not; "method choice" may in part be "in-domain training on an imbalanced corpus". Because language is confounded with sector here, and because the corpus texts are English paraphrases of multilingual sources (§3.7), that difference is reported per source-language stratum and not read as a cross-language ranking or a German-text result, the same discipline under which §5A.5 retracts the aggregate comparison. What is claimed is the equal-opportunity escalation result. In a deployed system, uneven accuracy across languages or channels would mean uneven service, a feedback-routing analogue of measurement and representation bias (Mehrabi et al., 2021). This argues for the contextual model path *and* for monitoring routing distributions across segments as a standing Responsible-AI control rather than a one-off check, consistent with Rakova et al.'s (2021) finding that responsible-AI practice has to move from reactive, incident-driven responses to structurally supported, proactive ones.

**Detection versus false accusation.** A late design cycle surfaced a tension the earlier controls did not: a customer asked for AI-generated verbatims to be flagged before triage. Building it required deciding whose error to prefer. The measurements are in §4.4; their shape is that the plausible detectors fail in the direction that matters most here, flagging genuine, articulate, angry complaints and doing so unevenly across languages. A feedback platform that quietly suppresses its most articulate critics has inverted its own purpose, and it would do so under a fairness failure of exactly the kind §5A.5 measures for triage. The resolution adopted is to report what provenance and structure can establish, to abstain explicitly where text length cannot support a judgement, and to decline the per-comment authorship claim altogether while publishing the measurements that justify declining it. This is the same discipline as the outcome estimator's refusal to fit, applied to a classification rather than a regression, and it suggests a general principle for governed systems: where a capability's errors fall unevenly on the people the system exists to serve, the defensible product decision may be to measure the capability and then not ship it.

**Regulation as a moving target, and as a ceiling that does not bind.** The regime consolidated during the build (§2.13; Appendix B), and the artifact's classification means most of it never bound the artifact at all: the Chapter III obligations attach to high-risk systems, and this one is not high-risk (§4.9). The design stance follows from that rather than from any deadline: human oversight, logging and transparency are adopted as durable commitments, in the spirit of the voluntary codes the Act invites (Article 95), because they are what makes an action-taking system defensible to its users, not because a date requires them. The consequence for the manuscript is a discipline of labelling: where Appendix B names an article, it says whether the row is an obligation or a commitment.

## 6.3 Competitive Positioning

Situating the artifact against the market clarifies its contribution. Two category-level shifts frame the analysis (as of July 2026). First, the *insight* half of the chain is being declared commoditised by the analysts themselves: Forrester retired its customer-feedback-management Wave and relaunched the category around real-time insight, reasoning and autonomous action (Fazio, 2025), with trust as the axis of its Q1 2026 landscape (Forrester, 2026), while the enterprise top consolidates into data-asset conglomerates (Qualtrics' $6.75B acquisition of Press Ganey Forsta; Qualtrics, 2026b). Second, adjacent suites are absorbing loop-closure as a *feature*: Amplitude's agentic analytics maps feedback themes from surveys and support tickets onto the behavioural data it already owns (Amplitude, 2026); Medallia's partnership with Ada executes policy-aware workflows from insight, with outcomes fed back into Medallia (Medallia, 2026); Sprinklr ships audit trails and agent evaluation (CMSWire, 2026a); and support suites now price on *verified resolutions* within the ticket channel (CMSWire, 2026b). Against this backdrop, the feedback-tooling landscape still divides into roles: **enterprise VoC suites** that collect and increasingly act; **product-feedback tools** (Productboard, Pendo, Canny) strong on "what to build"; and the closest group, **feedback-intelligence platforms** (Enterpret, Chattermill, Thematic, Dovetail, the last now shipping agents that "watch, enrich, broadcast, and trigger real work" to all paid plans, with no documented outcome measurement; Dovetail, 2025).

The decisive distinction is where each stops, and for the closest competitor this can now be stated on *verified* rather than marketed capabilities. A verification of Enterpret's marketed claims (July 2026; an LLM multi-agent exercise over public material, mostly Enterpret's own pages, in which 114 extracted claims were reduced to 25 testable ones, of which 21 were confirmed and 4 refuted by three-vote adversarial checking) confirmed genuine strength on the left half of the chain (50+ connectors, a five-level adaptive taxonomy with drift and duplicate detection, conversational querying, an MCP server, bidirectional ticket routing to Jira/Linear/Zendesk; security certifications are claimed on its security page and were not verified) and **refuted** the three autonomy claims most relevant to this thesis: its agents are not autonomous (human approval is required), and its adaptive taxonomy *proposes* rather than deploys changes. Its **outcome measurement could be neither confirmed nor refuted** from public material. "Closing the loop" there means notifying the original reporter when a fix ships, not proving the metric moved. On the evidence reviewed, no platform in the group binds actions to an **outcome contract** and verifies resolution, and none ships a **decaying learning repository** that accumulates *which actions resolved which problems in which contexts*. The consequence of these shifts is that no *single* differentiator survives scrutiny any longer. Behavioural-outcome linkage exists (Amplitude, within its own analytics), governance artefacts exist (Sprinklr, at enterprise scale), insight-to-execution exists (Medallia+Ada, via partnership), and SMB-priced agent action exists (Dovetail, with no documented outcome measurement). What remains unclaimed, on the evidence reviewed, is the **combination**: actions bound to outcome *contracts* and measured for recurrence, executed cross-system through an approval gate with an audit trail, with a decaying learning repository, deployable EU-sovereign, and reachable by mid-sized organisations rather than only the enterprise tier. That combination, not any component, is the artifact's defensible position. Gartner's (2025) prediction that over 40% of agentic-AI projects will be cancelled by 2027, citing escalating costs, *unclear business value and inadequate risk controls*, names two of the failure modes the combination addresses. It is also a deliberately *narrow* wedge: the artifact integrates with collection and delivery tools rather than competing with them, and it should not compete on support-channel loop closure, which helpdesks now close natively. Its scope is cross-system *execution*, measured against recurrence of the problem in the ingested feedback stream; business-metric outcomes are human-recorded and graded E (§3.5.5). There is a second, uncomfortable symmetry to note: the same verification standard applied to *this* artifact shows its own outcome measurement running on simulated outcome data so far (§6.4). The differentiator is designed and instrumented, not yet field-proven. *(This positioning is the thesis's analytical claim; it is not a market endorsement, and competitor capabilities evolve.)*

## 6.4 Limitations

Beyond the methodological limitations stated in Chapter 3, §3.10, the following bound the claims in the light of the results.

1. **The corpus and its labels.** The quantitative evaluation rests on a modest, balanced corpus of 188 signals whose seed labels were authored alongside the datasets (their provenance is stated in §3.7); the numbers characterise the pipeline on this corpus, not a population. Language is confounded with sector (the B2B stratum is almost entirely German-source, fintech overwhelmingly English-source), the texts are English paraphrases throughout with `language` recording the source review's language, and the German-source gold-escalate stratum is eight signals. An earlier cross-language classification claim was retracted rather than defended, and only escalation recall, which conditions on the gold label, is claimed (§5A.5). Two fields, `theme_seed` and `recommended_action_seed`, carry labels but no metric; the routing fields are scored only under a supplied closed inventory, an upper bound on free-form routing; and six of the 188 signals were lost to endpoint errors in the LLM run.

2. **Which model, and which prompt, was evaluated.** Every accuracy figure in Chapter 5 was produced on GLM-5.2, reached through a gateway the artifact's own residency classifier places as "unknown"; the configured production default is a different, EU-hosted model that has not been run on either gold set, and no self-hosted model has been run either, so the sovereignty commitment of §4.4 is an enforced control whose accuracy cost is unmeasured. Cost and latency per predictor, which §5A.6 says drive the deployment choice, are not reported. The §5A "contextual path" is a generic triage prompt on that model, not the artifact's enrichment stage; the production-path re-run (§5A.2) is the number that would characterise the deployed pipeline.

3. **The in-repo golden set.** It is a second gold standard, not an independent one: both strata were authored in the repository (the German stratum's authorship is documented; the English items' should be stated the same way), the optimisation and held-out halves are pooled in every published figure, the exemplar interventions were tuned on the optimisation half, the "three runs" behind the urgency claim are same-day re-runs of identical items that bound model noise rather than item sampling, and the exemplar set changed between the null at n = 80 and the significant result at n = 100. The hallucination heuristic is English-scope by construction, and the German tag metrics improved partly by construction when the rubric was completed. The 0.86 star-proxy figure, not 0.97, is the one to quote, and the headline paired test (p = 0.029) moves to 0.043 or 0.052 if a single item flips (§5A.6).

4. **The distinctive contribution is demonstrated, not measured.** §5A validates a precondition (triage quality). The learning loop's *influence* is measured (66.7% remedy adoption), but the probe varied retrieval, not learning age, so perishability itself (DP2) is unmeasured; the outcome data behind those learnings is simulated; no comparison against an existing system is reported, which Gregor and Hevner (2013) expect of an improvement contribution; and the per-industry weight profiles (DP6) have not been evaluated in any condition. Whether a measured outcome contract and a perishable memory improve *real* decisions awaits a live outcome contract on non-simulated data.

5. **The estimator's own caveats.** The interrupted-time-series fit uses ordinary least squares on daily rates with plain standard errors: autocorrelated counts are not modelled (heteroskedasticity-and-autocorrelation-consistent errors are the upgrade path), a count GLM would suit sparse series better, and when several actions on one theme overlap the earliest execution is attributed the effect. The detectability note is a two-sample bound that understates the estimator's own minimum detectable effect by an order of magnitude for the default contract (§3.5.5). "Governed" is precise but modest: a mandatory per-action approval gate with blocking policy checks, an append-only audit record with a hashed evidence pack, and a labelled model score, not a policy engine and not calibration machinery (Guo et al., 2017); the score orders cases without warranting a probability.

6. **The qualitative study and the researcher.** The practitioner study is small-N and design-validity oriented: it can evidence perceived usefulness and trust but cannot establish a causal effect on time-to-action or retention, and its trust items (TAM 9–12) are author-written and positively keyed (§3.10). The researcher is also the designer; the mitigations (standardised subscales, double-coding, a star-rating gold that is independent of the text, a reproducible harness) reduce but do not remove that bias.

7. **The artifact as a situated instantiation.** It pushes approved actions to real external systems and runs its outcome loop in production (§4.7); connector pulls are implemented against five official APIs, one of which (Trustpilot) awaits customer credentials to run live. A structured code review (July 2026) found ten high-severity defects (unauthenticated reads, client-side-only auth, row-level security declared but not enforced), since hardened (§4.9); the generalisation is that **declared controls are not enforced controls until adversarially verified**, and the build is not warranted production-multi-tenant. The external-review episode of Appendix F adds a limitation about evidence rather than reviewers: an external evaluation of an artifact evaluates what the artifact *shows*, so any assessment against a deployed surface understates capability that is built but unexposed, and this thesis's use of that episode inherits the bias.

## 6.5 Future Work

The evaluation and design point to four directions. The first has now largely landed and is restated in terms of what it left behind. **(1) Complete the evaluation's coverage.** A contextual LLM predictor was run against this thesis's 188-signal seed-label harness on 4 August 2026, closing the previously pending predictor row; what that run scored is a generic triage prompt on the same model, not the artifact's enrichment stage, so the two evidence streams are on comparable *model* footing but not yet on comparable *pipeline* footing. The production-path re-run (`predict_llm_production.py`, with pre-registered scoring rules; §5A.2) is the step that closes that gap. Golden-set growth has also run its course for now, from 60 to 100 items, with the exemplar effect significant on exact tag sets (8.75% → 28.75%, McNemar p = 0.001) and, at n = 100, on urgency as well (83% → 90%, p = 0.039). What that leaves is sharper than the original direction. Two gold-labelled fields, `theme_seed` and `recommended_action_seed`, are **still not scored at all**: the semantic-agreement procedure §3.5.2 designs for open-vocabulary fields has not been executed, and until it is, the pipeline's accuracy claims cover four fields out of six. The routing fields need scoring in the **free-form production condition**, not only under a supplied inventory, before their numbers describe the deployed system. And the German stratum remains **authored**, so acquiring natural German texts, which would make the corpus textually multilingual and simultaneously break the language/sector confound that forced the retraction in §5A.5, is now the highest-value corpus work, not mere growth. **(2) Finish closing the connector loop.** The *push* side is live (approved actions create real Jira/Slack artefacts, §4.7) and five pull connectors are implemented against official APIs; what remains is running each against a customer's live credentials, so closure is measured against real downstream systems end-to-end. **(3) Replace simulated outcomes with a live outcome contract.** This is the single most important next step: one action bound to a real, non-simulated metric and measured through its window. The measurement design for it is already shipped and tested in the artifact (§3.5.5): segmented-regression interrupted time series at the action date, with default-on contracts proposed at approval. As panel data accumulates, synthetic difference-in-differences (Arkhangelsky et al., 2021) is the stronger design, with the controlled-experiment paradigm (Kohavi, Tang, & Xu, 2020) reserved for deployments that can support it. Naive before/after deltas are reported only as labelled fallbacks, which is also the shipped estimator's own honesty behaviour on insufficient data (§3.5.5). **(4) Operationalise fairness monitoring** of routing distributions across segments and languages as a standing control. The ingestion authenticity review (§4.4) already computes its own flag rate per language stratum and warns when one stratum is flagged disproportionately, which is a first instance of the pattern; extending the same self-monitoring to routing and escalation distributions is the remaining work. Longer-term, the adaptive taxonomy (§4.3) and a longitudinal field deployment measuring actual retention lift would test the design's value in situ.

## 6.6 Practical Implications

For practitioners, the central implication is that closing the feedback loop is less an analytics problem than a **workflow-and-governance** problem. The binding constraints are ownership, routing, measured closure, and retained memory, which are the stages this artifact makes first-class. For organisations adopting AI in customer operations, the work illustrates that **human oversight and auditability can be built in as durable design commitments** without abandoning automation, and that **triage accuracy must be evaluated, not assumed**: naive methods fail on real operational feedback (here, English paraphrases of English- and German-language sources), and even modest learned models materially help. Two further implications follow from the results and should be stated plainly. First, the cheaper route to severity routing, a learned local model, is the configuration this evaluation found to be the equity-worst escalator (§5A.5): choosing it is a margin decision with a fairness cost, and it needs per-stratum monitoring of who gets escalated, not a one-off accuracy check. Second, routing value presupposes configuration: the journey-stage and owner figures of §5A.4.1 were obtained only with the workspace's inventory supplied, so an organisation adopting a system of this kind should expect to supply its owner list and journey taxonomy before routing drafts are worth reviewing.
