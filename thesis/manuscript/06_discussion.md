# Chapter 6: Discussion

This chapter turns the design and the bounded evaluation into transferable knowledge: design principles (RQ1, RQ2), the Responsible-AI tensions the work surfaced, the artifact's position against the products reviewed, one account of the limitations and an ordered agenda. Consistent with the design-science stance (Gregor & Hevner, 2013), the contribution is offered as nascent design theory intended to transfer to other feedback-to-action systems, not as knowledge that practitioners have yet confirmed.

## 6.1 Design Principles

The design decisions of Chapter 4 (§4.2 to §4.4) generalise into five design principles and two implementation decisions. Labels DP1 to DP7 are kept for traceability with Appendix D; DP3 and DP6 are engineering choices rather than contributions; DP7 is abstracted from the design cycle of §4.4. Table 6.1 states each principle's context, mechanism, expected consequence, evidence status (vocabulary of §1.7) and boundary. No row claims that a principle improves practitioner decisions; that evidence was not collected.

DP2 is restated in full because its status is the most easily overstated:

> CLARA reduces a learning's retrieval weight with age using an explicit half-life. Small probes show that retrieval can alter recommendations on GLM-5.2, but this effect did not reproduce on the production-default model. The probes did not vary learning age or assess recommendation quality, and they ran on simulation-authored learnings over simulated outcome data, a condition the retrieval gate no longer admits. Decay is therefore an implemented design hypothesis whose benefit remains untested.

Why not an agent. The objection that a capable language-model agent could route, ticket and follow up unaided, making the approval interrupt friction dressed as governance, meets the routing evidence of Table 6.1 first: even with the workspace's own inventory supplied (§5A.4.1), an unattended agent would misroute many problems quietly. The gate's defence carries its limit: it controls false positives, wrong or harmful actions reaching an external system, and it cannot recover escalation-worthy signals the enrichment ranks too low, 22 of 50 under the seed labels on the production rubric (§5A.4.2). An agent inside an issue tracker reduced engineers' effort when steered rather than left to act (Takerngsaksiri et al., 2024), and Gartner (2025) expects many agentic-AI projects to be cancelled for unclear business value and inadequate risk controls, the failures a draft-and-approve loop is built to avoid. The bounded design (DP4, DP5) is the configuration the accuracy evidence supports; if routing evidence ever justified unattended action for some consequence class, DP4's graduation is where it would be expressed.

## 6.2 Responsible-AI Reflections and Tensions

Oversight versus throughput. Article 14-style human approval is the artifact's central safeguard, and every gate is friction. Graduating only the depth of review (§4.3.1) is a value judgement about which actions warrant two approvers; the practitioner study designed to learn whether that boundary is trusted or merely slow (H6, H7) was not conducted. Oversight can also be nominal, since awareness-based measures are unlikely to de-bias overseers (Laux & Ruschemeier, 2025). The gate therefore claims only what §6.1 states: it controls false positives, since in the current build (§4.1) nothing reaches an external system without an attributable approval bound to the reviewed outbound content, and it cannot recover escalation-worthy signals the enrichment ranks too low. The bound reviewer identity, the opt-in four-eyes mode that rejects self-confirmation and the tested works-council attestation (§4.4) answer nominal oversight by design (Siebert et al., 2023); whether reviewers rubber-stamp awaits monitoring of approval records (§4.4) and the study not yet run.

Auditability versus simplicity. A full audit trail (Article 12; GDPR Article 22) makes automated action accountable and adds load. The design bet is that visibility of why an action fired increases appropriate trust (Lee & See, 2004), extended to disclosure of what kind of number is shown: the model score is labelled an uncalibrated heuristic, every outcome readout carries an evidence grade, every measurement discloses its provenance (§4.4), and guardrails without a data source say so. Each adds load, but it moves the trust question from whether the user believes the number to whether the user knows what kind of number it is; the practitioner study whose TAM items would test the bet was not conducted.

Accuracy, fairness and language. On the eight German-source signals whose seed label warrants escalation, TF-IDF + logistic regression escalated 5 of 8 against 39 of 41 English-source positives, the generic prompt 7 of 8 against 36 of 41, and the production stage 5 of 8 against 22 of 41 (§5A.5). These are descriptive figures: a significant within-predictor test beside a non-significant one does not compare the two gaps (Gelman & Stern, 2006), the learned model's training on an imbalanced corpus is a rival explanation, language is confounded with sector, and the texts are English paraphrases, so nothing here measures German-text processing. What is claimed is a descriptive difference in escalation recall across source strata on this corpus; no predictor is ranked and no language effect is inferred. Uneven recall across languages or channels would mean uneven service, a routing analogue of measurement and representation bias (Mehrabi et al., 2021), so the implication is a standing monitoring control on escalation and routing distributions, with denominators, whichever predictor is deployed (Rakova et al., 2021).

Detection versus false accusation. The candidate AI-authorship detectors flagged genuine complaints unevenly across languages and skewed toward angrier ones (mean 2.21 stars against 2.70; §4.4); a platform that flags its angriest genuine critics has inverted its purpose, hence DP7.

Regulation as a ceiling that does not bind. The regime consolidated during the build (§2.6; Appendix B), and most of it never bound the artifact, which is not high-risk (§4.9). Human oversight, logging and transparency are adopted as durable commitments in the spirit of the voluntary codes the Act invites (Article 95). For the articles it tabulates, Appendix B says whether each row is an obligation or a commitment.

## 6.3 Competitive Positioning

Two shifts frame the positioning as of July 2026. The insight half of the chain is being declared commoditised: Forrester relaunched the category around real-time insight, reasoning and autonomous action (Fazio & Evelson, 2025), with trust as the axis of its 2026 landscape (Fazio & Evelson, 2026). Adjacent suites are absorbing loop closure as a feature: Amplitude maps feedback themes onto its own behavioural data (Amplitude, 2026), Medallia and Ada execute workflows from insight (Medallia, 2026), Sprinklr documents audit trails and agent evaluation (CMSWire, 2026a), support suites price on resolutions confirmed within the ticket channel (CMSWire, 2026b), and Dovetail ships agents that trigger work without documented outcome measurement (Dovetail, 2025). The landscape still divides into enterprise VoC suites, product-feedback tools (Productboard, Pendo, Canny) and feedback-intelligence platforms (Enterpret, Chattermill, Thematic, Dovetail).

For the closest competitor, Enterpret, an assistant-led claim check over public material, mostly its own pages, was run in July 2026. Its strength on the left half of the chain (connectors, adaptive taxonomy, conversational querying, bidirectional ticket routing) is documented in the sources reviewed; its marketed autonomy is contradicted by its own documentation, which requires human approval and describes the taxonomy as proposing rather than deploying changes; its outcome measurement is not documented, since closing the loop there means notifying the reporter when a fix ships, not showing that the metric moved. Within the literature and product documentation reviewed for this chapter (references; §2.7 and this section), no evaluated system was identified that combines actions bound to outcome contracts and measured for recurrence, cross-system execution through an approval gate with an audit trail, a decaying learning repository, EU-sovereign deployment and mid-market reach. The position is therefore the combination, and a narrow wedge: the artifact integrates with collection and delivery tools rather than competing with them, leaves support-channel loop closure to the helpdesks, and measures cross-system execution against recurrence in the ingested feedback stream; business-metric outcomes are human-recorded and graded E (§3.5.5). By the same standard, this artifact's outcome measurement has run on simulated outcome data so far (§6.4): the differentiator is designed and instrumented, not field-proven. ## 6.4 Limitations

Beyond §3.10, these bound the claims.

1. The corpus and its labels. The evaluation rests on 188 English paraphrases of public reviews whose seed labels were drafted alongside the datasets by a language-model assistant (§3.7). The only agreement check, a second rating of 40 risk-labelled items (κ = 0.55, 0.38–0.72, unweighted; 0.70, 0.56–0.82, linear-weighted), is reported as second-rating agreement whose independent human provenance is unverified and is not used as human validation (Appendix G.1). Star ratings are a human-origin sentiment proxy; neither reference standard is human-labelled. Language is confounded with sector, the German-source escalation-worthy stratum has eight cases, and escalation recall, which conditions on the seed label, is the only stratum comparison carried with a test; the per-slice sentiment figures are descriptive (§5A.5). Theme and recommended action carry labels but no metric; journey stage and owner are scored only with a supplied closed inventory, a constrained task rather than a bound on free-form routing, on 182 signals after 6 were lost to endpoint errors.

2. Which model and which prompt. The generic-prompt figures and the in-repo golden-set snapshot were produced on GLM-5.2, whose residency the artifact classifies as unknown and whose risk labels shifted between the August and September runs (§5A.4.2). The production stage on GLM-5.2 and on Mistral Small 4 (the production default) reached sentiment 0.83 and 0.84 and risk 0.50 and 0.59, not distinguishable on this corpus after the retrospective Holm correction, in a comparison where model, batch size and run conditions differ together (§5A.4.2; Appendix G.6). The matched rerun that would isolate a model effect has not been run, no self-hosted model has been run on either gold set, and latency and cost are not reported. The risk agreement of 0.50–0.59 may reflect a difference between the risk and urgency constructs that the corpus cannot separate from conservative calibration.

3. Statistical status of the comparisons. No comparison was declared primary before the runs, so every contrast is exploratory in the statistical sense, corrected retrospectively within one family of ten comparisons per task (§5A.6). The generic prompt's sentiment lead over TF-IDF + logistic regression (0.86 against 0.78) is stable across repeated runs on the same items and not significant after the correction (Holm-adjusted p = 0.174); on risk the two are not distinguishable on this corpus, and the generic prompt's repeats (0.65–0.66) do not reproduce its run-0 figure of 0.72. The in-repo golden set is a development and validation benchmark co-developed with the artifact, not independent confirmation; its production-default and exemplar A/B figures rest on contemporaneous records without a preserved per-item record (§5A.7; Appendix G.4).

4. The distinctive contribution is demonstrated, not measured. §5A measures (bounded) a precondition, triage quality. The retrieval probes (§5A.7; contemporaneous records) varied retrieval, not learning age, so perishability (DP2) is unmeasured, and the outcome data behind the learnings is simulated. No comparison against an existing system is reported, as Gregor and Hevner (2013) expect of an improvement contribution, and the weight profiles (DP6) are unevaluated.

5. The estimator's own caveats. The interrupted-time-series fit uses ordinary least squares on daily rates with plain standard errors: autocorrelation, seasonality and time-varying confounding are not modelled (Bernal et al., 2017), and overlapping actions on one theme are attributed to the earliest. A fitted model is not a validated causal design, and randomisation is infeasible in the single-organisation pilot deployments considered here. The worsening-threshold guardrail (relative worsening above 20%) is a check, not a statistical test. "Governed" is modest: an approval gate with blocking policy checks, an append-only audit record with a hashed evidence pack and a labelled model score, not a policy engine or calibration machinery (Guo et al., 2017).

6. The practitioner study and the researcher. The practitioner study was not conducted, so RQ4 is unanswered, the friction clause of RQ2 is open, and no claim about perceived usefulness or trust is made (§3.10). The researcher is also the designer; standardised subscales, a star-rating gold independent of the text, a reproducible harness and planned double-coding reduce but do not remove that bias.

7. The artifact as a situated instantiation. The platform has run in production since July 2026 and pushes approved actions to real external systems, but the outcome contracts, loop verdicts, learning memory and dispatch binding were implemented and tested at commits not deployed at the time of writing (§4.1; Appendix G.8). The July 2026 code review found controls declared but not enforced, since hardened (§4.9; Appendix G.8): declared controls are not enforced controls until adversarially tested, and the build is not warranted production-multi-tenant. An external evaluation assesses what the artifact shows (Appendix F), so it understates capability that is built but unexposed.

## 6.5 Future Work

Five items, in order of what they would change.

1. Run the practitioner study. Instruments, reporting tiers and the survey script are ready (§5B; Appendix A). Until sessions are held, RQ4 is unanswered and RQ2's friction clause is open.

2. Run the human annotation study on the existing corpus, with a provenance audit of the seed labels first: two independent raters label sentiment, risk, urgency, journey stage and owner blind, disagreements are adjudicated, every committed prediction file is re-scored without a model call, and the learned baseline is retrained on the adjudicated gold (`evaluation/annotation_kit.py`). This would indicate how much of the 0.50–0.59 risk agreement reflects calibration and how much construct disagreement; it does not establish that the seed labels were correct.

3. Compare no memory, memory without decay and memory with decay on frozen scenarios, with blind assessment of recommendation quality across several half-lives, replacing the retrieval probes of §5A.7 with a test of DP2.

4. Bind one action to a live outcome contract on non-simulated data and measure it through its window (§3.5.5), preceded by a retrospective read of the shipped estimator over real dated inflow around a known event (`evaluation/retrospective_its.py`), which describes rather than attributes; synthetic difference-in-differences (Arkhangelsky et al., 2021) or a controlled experiment (Kohavi, Tang, & Xu, 2020) awaits deployments that can support it.

5. Operationalise source-stratum fairness monitoring of escalation and routing distributions, with denominators, as a standing control; the authenticity gate's per-language flag rate (§4.4) is the first instance.

## 6.6 Practical Implications

For practitioners, closing the feedback loop is a workflow-and-governance problem before it is an analytics problem: ownership, routing, measured closure and retained memory are the stages this artifact makes first-class. Human oversight and auditability can be built in as durable design commitments without abandoning automation, and enrichment accuracy must be evaluated rather than assumed: on this corpus the learned local model and the language-model paths were not distinguishable on sentiment after correction. Whichever route to severity is chosen, escalation recall should be monitored per source stratum in deployment, with denominators; the eight-case stratum here supports a monitoring control, not a choice of predictor (§5A.5). Routing value presupposes configuration: the figures of §5A.4.1 were obtained with the workspace's inventory supplied, so an adopting organisation should supply its owner list and journey taxonomy before routing drafts are worth reviewing.
