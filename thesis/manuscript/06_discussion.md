# Chapter 6: Discussion

This chapter synthesises the design and evaluation into transferable knowledge. It abstracts the artifact's design decisions into **design principles** (RQ1, RQ2), reflects on the **Responsible-AI tensions** the work surfaced, positions the artifact against the **competitive landscape**, and states the **limitations** and **future work**. Consistent with the design-science stance (Gregor & Hevner, 2013), the contribution is offered as nascent design theory — prescriptive knowledge that should transfer to other feedback-to-action systems — not as a universal law.

## 6.1 Design Principles

The five non-trivial design decisions (Chapter 4, §4.3), together with the architectural stance (§4.2), generalise into six design principles for governed feedback-to-action systems. Each is stated with its **cost**, because a design principle that buys something for nothing is usually a platitude.

**DP1 — Make closure a contract, not a side effect.** Bind every action, at creation, to an explicit outcome metric and measurement window, and distinguish operational, customer, and outcome closure; this is what lets the system claim a loop *closed* rather than merely *acted* (Bone et al., 2017; Ghasemaghaei et al., 2017). The field evidence that recovery actions can have null or *negative* effects (Halperin et al., 2022) makes this principle load-bearing rather than decorative: without measurement, a feedback-to-action system can industrialise harm as efficiently as help. *Cost:* it forces teams to commit to a success metric up front — exactly the discipline most lack — and a closure metric can be gamed by choosing an easy one, so the contract governs only as well as the metric is chosen.

**DP2 — Treat organisational memory as perishable.** Model learnings as decaying and inspectable (confidence decay over a half-life, with staleness flags) so past results inform but do not ossify present decisions (Argote, 2013; Walsh & Ungson, 1991; for the prior art in LLM decay-memory and the precise novelty boundary, see §4.3.2 and Zhong et al., 2024). This principle has moved from argued to *partially measured*: in the artifact's instrumented evaluation, retrieving stored learnings raised the share of a past remedy appearing in new recommendations from 21% to 71% (§5A.7) — the memory demonstrably steers recommendations — though the outcomes behind those learnings remain simulated, so *whether the steered recommendation is better* is still open. *Cost:* the half-life is a parameter the organisation rarely knows; set it wrong and the system discards knowledge that still holds or trusts knowledge that has died. Decay encodes *plausible* forgetting, not *correct* forgetting.

**DP3 — Let severity emerge from the set, not the loudest member.** Compute priority by fusing the corroborating set — strongest urgency, volume, negative proportion — rather than any single signal (Bleiholder & Naumann, 2008), mitigating both single-loud-complaint over-escalation and the dilution of widespread, low-urgency problems. *Cost:* fusion can bury the rare, catastrophic single signal — the lone fraud report or safety defect — that volume-weighting is structurally inclined to under-rank, so the rule needs an explicit override for low-frequency, high-severity cases.

**DP4 — Graduate automation authority and make conflicts explicit.** Resolve competing directives by transparent, ordered rules (priority → specificity → action-type dedupe) and expose *which* rule won and *why*, with an `auto_execute`/`pending_approval` boundary, so autonomy is bounded and auditable rather than emergent (Parasuraman et al., 2000). *Cost:* every rule and tier is configuration debt; ordered resolution faithfully entrenches a *wrong* high-priority rule as readily as a right one, and the transparency only pays off if someone actually maintains the rule set.

**DP5 — Keep the model bounded and the loop deterministic around it.** Confine language-model reasoning to named stages behind a single interface, with deterministic code for validation, conflict resolution, execution, and audit (§4.2); the §5A finding that triage accuracy is strongly method-dependent reinforces that the model is a component to be contained, not a controller to be trusted. *Cost:* bounding the model caps its ceiling — you forgo the open-ended flexibility of a free-roaming agent and you must build and maintain the deterministic scaffolding yourself, trading adaptivity for governability and testability.

**DP6 — Adapt by configuration, not retraining.** Where triage priorities genuinely differ by context (sector, market), encode the difference as inspectable configuration — named weight profiles over a fixed scoring model (§4.3.5) — rather than as per-context fine-tuned models. The cross-industry evaluation supports the premise: the same pipeline produced sector-appropriate vocabularies on fintech, food-delivery, and B2B-manufacturing feedback with no per-sector training (§5A.7). *Cost:* profiles are authored priors — they can encode the designer's sector stereotypes as easily as sector expertise, and every profile is configuration to maintain; inspectability is the safeguard, not a guarantee.

## 6.2 Responsible-AI Reflections and Tensions

Treating Responsible AI as an integral design dimension (rather than a compliance layer) surfaced genuine tensions worth naming.

**Oversight versus throughput.** EU AI Act Article 14-style human approval is the artifact's central safeguard, but every approval gate is friction. The design response — letting rules declare `auto_execute` and reserving approval for risk — is itself a value judgement about which actions are consequential, and the qualitative study (H6, H7) is designed precisely to learn whether practitioners find that boundary trustworthy. There is no neutral default here: more oversight is safer and slower, and the *right* setting is contextual. The deeper tension is that oversight can be *nominal*: legal analysis of Article 14 argues its awareness-based measures are unlikely to de-bias human overseers given the automation-bias evidence (Laux & Ruschemeier, 2025), so an approval gate earns its safety claim only through design — friction proportional to risk, visible evidence and an honestly-labelled model score, and per-decision attributability (Siebert et al., 2023) — and through monitoring for rubber-stamping (§4.9), not through the gate's mere existence. The July 2026 revision hardened the gate in exactly that spirit: the approving reviewer's identity is now bound to the verified login (a JWT-derived pseudonym) rather than self-asserted in the request body — previously any editor could sign as someone else; an opt-in **four-eyes** mode requires two distinct approvers for consequential actions and rejects a reviewer confirming their own approval; and the German works-council pack carries a code-verifiable *no-individual-performance-monitoring* attestation backed by a CI test that calls every read endpoint as a non-admin and fails if any person-capable field leaks — the oversight claim held as continuously executed evidence rather than documentation, with its residual limits (the designated admin role still sees identities; admin-access logging is not yet built) stated in the attestation itself.

**Auditability versus simplicity.** A full audit trail (Article 12; GDPR Article 22) is what makes automated action accountable, but it adds interface and cognitive load. The design bet is that visibility of *why* an action fired (which rule, which signal, which outcome) increases appropriate trust (Lee & See, 2004); the TAM trust items test that bet. The July 2026 revision extended the bet from explanation to **honesty as a feature**: the UI's "confidence" label was renamed **model score** and explained as an uncalibrated heuristic rather than a validated probability; every outcome readout now carries an **evidence grade** (A–E) that grades the measurement *design*, from randomized holdout down to manual assertion; each measurement discloses its **provenance** (instrumented versus "unverified manual observation" — stamped server-side, so clients cannot spoof it); guardrails without a data source surface an explicit "no data source" marker instead of silence; and near-duplicate signals are annotated, never dropped. Each of these adds interface load — the tension stands — but they shift the trust question from *whether the user believes the number* to *whether the user knows what kind of number it is*.

**Accuracy, fairness, and language.** The evaluation made a fairness concern concrete: lexicon triage is markedly less reliable in English than German on this corpus and varies by sector (§5A.5). In a deployed system, uneven accuracy across languages or channels would mean uneven service — a feedback-routing analogue of measurement and representation bias (Mehrabi et al., 2021). This argues for the contextual model path *and* for monitoring routing distributions across segments as a standing Responsible-AI control, not a one-off check (Rakova et al., 2021).

**Regulation as a moving target.** The compliance knowledge base must track a consolidating regime — the 2026 Digital Omnibus postponed the Annex III high-risk obligations to December 2027 while leaving the Article 50 transparency obligations on their August 2026 schedule (only the Article 50(2) watermarking sub-obligation gained a grace period to December 2026) (Chapter 4, §4.4; Appendix B). That the deadline a system targets can move by a year mid-build is itself the argument for the design stance taken here: treat human oversight, logging, and transparency as durable commitments, not as features triggered by a date.

## 6.3 Competitive Positioning

Situating the artifact against the market clarifies its contribution. Two category-level shifts frame the analysis (as of July 2026). First, the *insight* half of the chain is being declared commoditised by the analysts themselves: Forrester retired its customer-feedback-management Wave and relaunched the category around real-time reasoning, autonomous action, and trust (Forrester, 2026), while the enterprise top consolidates into data-asset conglomerates (Qualtrics' $6.75B acquisition of Press Ganey Forsta; Qualtrics, 2026b). Second, adjacent suites are absorbing loop-closure as a *feature*: Amplitude's agentic analytics ties feedback themes to behavioural and revenue data it already owns (Amplitude, 2026); Medallia's partnership with Ada executes policy-aware workflows from insight, with published resolution gains (Medallia, 2026); Sprinklr ships audit trails and agent evaluation (CMSWire, 2026a); and support suites now price on *verified resolutions* within the ticket channel (CMSWire, 2026b). Against this backdrop, the feedback-tooling landscape still divides into roles: **enterprise VoC suites** that collect and increasingly act; **product-feedback tools** (Productboard, Pendo, Canny) strong on "what to build"; and the closest group, **feedback-intelligence platforms** (Enterpret, Chattermill, Thematic, Dovetail — the last now shipping agents that "trigger real work" at SMB price points without documented approval gates or outcome measurement; Dovetail, 2025).

The decisive distinction is where each stops, and for the closest competitor this can now be stated on *verified* rather than marketed capabilities. A structured, multi-source verification of Enterpret's claims (July 2026; 114 extracted claims, 25 surviving three-vote adversarial verification) confirmed genuine strength on the left half of the chain — 50+ connectors, a five-level adaptive taxonomy with drift and duplicate detection, conversational querying, an MCP server, bidirectional ticket routing to Jira/Linear/Zendesk, and a mature certification posture — but **refuted** three of the claims most relevant to this thesis: its agents are not autonomous (human approval is required), its adaptive taxonomy *proposes* rather than deploys changes, and its **outcome measurement could not be verified at all**. "Closing the loop" there means notifying the original reporter when a fix ships — not proving the metric moved. On the evidence reviewed, no platform in the group binds actions to an **outcome contract** and verifies resolution, and none ships a **decaying learning repository** that accumulates *which actions resolved which problems in which contexts*. The consequence of these shifts is that no *single* differentiator survives scrutiny any longer: behavioural-outcome linkage exists (Amplitude, within its own analytics), governance artefacts exist (Sprinklr, at enterprise scale), insight-to-execution exists (Medallia+Ada, via partnership), and SMB-priced agent action exists (Dovetail, ungoverned). What remains unclaimed — on the evidence reviewed — is the **combination**: actions bound to outcome *contracts* and verified for resolution, executed cross-system through an approval gate with an audit trail, with a decaying learning repository, deployable EU-sovereign, at a price accessible to 10–500-employee companies (the closest competitor's reported entry price is ≈$12k/year; Capterra, 2026 — a more honest anchor than the enterprise price points cited earlier in this literature). That combination, not any component, is the artifact's defensible position — and Gartner's (2025) prediction that over 40% of agentic-AI projects will be cancelled by 2027 for *unclear business value and inadequate risk controls* names exactly the two failure modes the combination addresses. It is also a deliberately *narrow* wedge: the artifact integrates with collection and delivery tools rather than competing with them, and it should not compete on support-channel loop closure, which helpdesks now close natively — its scope is *cross-system* outcomes. There is a second, uncomfortable symmetry to note: the same verification standard applied to *this* artifact shows its own outcome measurement running on simulated outcome data so far (§6.4) — the differentiator is designed and instrumented, not yet field-proven. *(This positioning is the thesis's analytical claim; it is not a market endorsement, and competitor capabilities evolve.)*

## 6.4 Limitations

Beyond the methodological limitations stated in Chapter 3, §3.10, six are worth restating in light of the results. First, the quantitative evaluation rests on a **modest, balanced corpus** with human-authored seed labels; the numbers characterise the pipeline on this corpus, not a population. The LLM path was run against this thesis's own 188-signal seed-label harness on 4 August 2026, so the two evidence streams are now directly comparable — and what the comparison shows is a **divergence that should be read as a caution, not a corroboration**: the same enrichment path scores 0.86 sentiment accuracy against the independent star-rating proxy and 0.97 against the artifact's own curated golden set (§5A.7). The gap is expected, because a curated set selects for label clarity while the star proxy inherits every ambiguity of real raters, but it establishes that **the lower figure is the defensible one to quote**, and that in-repo numbers characterise the harness as much as the pipeline. The in-repo golden set, grown to 100 items by July 2026, carries its own disclosed caveats: its 28-item German stratum is **authored, not naturally occurring** (the source datasets' texts turned out to be English paraphrases); the hallucination heuristic is **EN-scope by construction** — grounded in the English tag vocabulary, it would mis-flag correct German tags, so non-English items are omitted from that metric rather than mis-scored; the German tag metrics improved partly *by construction* when the DE gold was completed to the two-tag rubric, which is disclosed in the published provenance and makes tag figures non-comparable across that boundary; and cross-run comparisons carry a model-noise caveat (the model is non-deterministic even at temperature 0), with the in-run paired A/B as the designed inference.

The completed run also introduced limitations of its own, which bound §5A more tightly than the pre-run text anticipated. **Language is confounded with sector** in this corpus — the B2B stratum is almost entirely German, fintech overwhelmingly English — so an aggregate "German versus English" classification comparison would largely restate the sector composition; an earlier cross-language fairness claim was accordingly **retracted** rather than defended, and only escalation recall, which conditions on the gold label, is claimed (§5A.5). That claim in turn rests on a **German gold-escalate stratum of eight signals**, an interval too wide to establish parity in general — it establishes that method choice governs escalation equity *on this corpus*. The routing fields are scored only under a **supplied closed inventory**, which makes 0.59 and 0.40 an *upper* bound on production free-form routing rather than an estimate of it, and six of the 188 signals were lost to endpoint errors during the run. And two fields — `theme_seed` and `recommended_action_seed` — carry gold labels for the full corpus but are **not scored at all**, so no accuracy claim extends to them. Second, the qualitative study is **small-N and design-validity oriented**: it can evidence perceived usefulness and trust but cannot establish a causal effect on time-to-action or retention. Third, the **researcher-as-designer** risk is mitigated (standardised instruments, double-coding, externally-labelled gold set, reproducible harness) but not eliminated. Fourth, the artifact is a **situated instantiation**: it pushes approved actions to real external systems and runs its outcome loop in production (§4.7), while connector *pulls* beyond Zendesk remain scaffolds — claims are about the design concept, not a finished product. Fifth, a structured code review of the platform (July 2026) found ten high-severity defects — unauthenticated reads, client-side-only auth, row-level security declared but not enforced — subsequently hardened (§4.9); the honest generalisation is that **declared controls are not enforced controls until adversarially verified**, and the build is not warranted production-multi-tenant. The external-review episode reported in §5A.8 complicates that lesson in one specific way worth carrying into the limitations: the reviews were substantially right about the trust and credibility layer and materially wrong about capabilities that existed but were not surfaced in the deployment. The limitation this establishes is about evidence, not about reviewers — **an external evaluation of an artifact evaluates what the artifact shows**, so any assessment conducted against a deployed surface systematically understates capability that is built but unexposed, and this thesis's own use of that episode inherits the bias. Sixth, and most consequential, the **distinctive contribution is still not fully measured**: §5A validates a precondition (triage quality), and the learning loop's *influence* is now measured (66.7% remedy adoption, §5A.7), but the outcome data behind those learnings is simulated — so whether a measured outcome contract and a perishable memory improve *real* decisions awaits a live outcome contract on non-simulated data. Relatedly, "governed" here is precise but modest — an `auto_execute`/approval gate, an immutable audit log, and an *advisory* compliance checker — not a formal policy engine; the stronger governance the framing invites (policy-as-code, automated routing-fairness enforcement) is design-forward, not yet built. The same modesty applies to calibration: renaming "confidence" to **model score** is honest labelling, not measurement — no calibration machinery (Guo et al., 2017) sits behind the heuristic, so the score orders cases without warranting a probability.

## 6.5 Future Work

The evaluation and design point to four directions. The first has now largely landed and is restated in terms of what it left behind. **(1) Complete the evaluation's coverage.** Unifying the two LLM evidence streams is done: the enrichment path was run against this thesis's 188-signal seed-label harness on 4 August 2026, closing the previously pending predictor row and putting both streams on comparable footing (§5A.3–5A.6). Golden-set growth has also run its course for now — 60 to 100 items, with the exemplar effect significant on exact tag sets (8.75% → 28.75%, McNemar p = 0.001) and, at n = 100, on urgency as well (83% → 90%, p = 0.039). What that leaves is sharper than the original direction. Two gold-labelled fields, `theme_seed` and `recommended_action_seed`, are **still not scored at all**: the semantic-agreement procedure §3.5.2 designs for open-vocabulary fields has not been executed, and until it is, the pipeline's accuracy claims cover four fields out of six. The routing fields need scoring in the **free-form production condition**, not only under a supplied inventory, before their numbers describe the deployed system. And the German stratum remains **authored**, so acquiring natural German texts — which would simultaneously break the language/sector confound that forced the retraction in §5A.5 — is now the highest-value corpus work, not mere growth. **(2) Finish closing the connector loop** — the *push* side is live (approved actions create real Jira/Slack artefacts, §4.7); what remains is broadening pulls beyond Zendesk, so closure is measured against real downstream systems end-to-end. **(3) Replace simulated outcomes with a live outcome contract** — the single most important next step: one action bound to a real, non-simulated metric and measured through its window. The measurement design for this is already shipped and tested in the artifact (§3.5.5): segmented-regression interrupted time series at the action date, with default-on contracts proposed at approval. As panel data accumulates, synthetic difference-in-differences (Arkhangelsky et al., 2021) is the stronger design, with the controlled-experiment paradigm (Kohavi, Tang, & Xu, 2020) reserved for deployments that can support it; naive before/after deltas are reported only as labelled fallbacks — which is also the shipped estimator's own honesty behaviour on insufficient data (§3.5.5). **(4) Operationalise fairness monitoring** of routing distributions across segments and languages as a standing control. Longer-term, the adaptive taxonomy (§4.3) and a longitudinal field deployment measuring actual retention lift would test the design's value in situ.

## 6.6 Practical Implications

For practitioners, the central implication is that closing the feedback loop is less an analytics problem than a **workflow-and-governance** problem: the binding constraints are ownership, routing, measured closure, and retained memory — precisely the stages this artifact makes first-class. For organisations adopting AI in customer operations, the work illustrates that **human oversight and auditability can be built in as durable design commitments** without abandoning automation, and that **triage accuracy must be evaluated, not assumed** — naive methods fail on real multilingual feedback, and even modest learned models materially help.
