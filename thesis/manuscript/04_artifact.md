# Chapter 4: The Artifact

## 4.1 Overview

The artifact is a working web platform that instantiates the Signal → Insight → Action → Learning cycle. This chapter documents what was built and the design decisions that carry the design-science contribution (Hevner, March, Park, & Ram, 2004, Guideline 1). CLARA is a Next.js front end on a FastAPI backend, with LangGraph orchestrating the language-model stages (the approval step is an interrupt node), Postgres for storage (Appendix E) and provider-agnostic model routing (`ai.py`). Mistral Small 4 (the production default) is a hosted European model; the generic-prompt figures and the published golden-set snapshot of 6 September 2026 were produced on GLM-5.2 through a gateway, and the production stage and the golden set have also been scored on the production default (§5A.4.2, §5A.7).

The platform has run in production since July 2026: front end on Vercel, API in Docker on a European VPS, database in the EU. The claim is dated against the outage record of 15 September 2026 (`docs/reviews/2026-09-15-api-outage-record.md`; Appendix G.8). On that day the serving API image was three weeks old, so built in the last week of August 2026; its commit is not recorded because the image carried no build identity, and it therefore predates every commit from 2 September onward. The following are implemented and regression-tested at the cited commits but were not deployed at the time of writing: persisted theme insights as candidates, the Postgres learning memory that synthesis retrieves from, theme-scoped outcome contracts and loop verdicts recorded by closing checkpoints (bbcd8b2 and migration 013, 2 September); the readiness route and audit hardening (01ced20, 2 September); identifier redaction at the model boundary (4bd1af8, 5 September); loop verdicts bound to observations, versioned contracts and the measurement clock at dispatch or implementation (3c286db and migration 016, 12 September); frozen contract terms, fixed observation intervals and explicit grades (be86add and migration 017, 13 September); and the binding of dispatch to the reviewed outbound content (d29b8b6, 13 September). The 12 September response (`docs/reviews/codex-2026-09-12-response.md`) had read the `/ready` 404 as a version lag; the outage record establishes that the 15 September event was an outage caused by host DNS and that the uptime workflow could not distinguish the two. Below, "deployed" and "in production" refer to the July platform and to what that record shows was live; the mechanisms above are implemented and tested in the current build.

![Figure 4.1: Implementation architecture: CLARA (Next.js + FastAPI + LangGraph, with a human-approval interrupt node).](../diagrams/rendered/03_architecture_clara.png)

The platform is not a feedback dashboard: its contribution is the governed path from a raw signal to a measured outcome and a recorded learning, where the literature locates the insight-action gap (Homburg & Fürst, 2005; Wirtz et al., 2010) and knowledge loss (Walsh & Ungson, 1991; Argote, 2013).

## 4.2 Architecture

The platform is a pipeline of bounded stages (Figure 4.2).

![Figure 4.2: Logical, build-agnostic architecture: governed loop with cross-cutting Responsible-AI controls.](../diagrams/rendered/01_architecture_logical.png)

| Stage | Responsibility | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Ingestion | Capture signals from existing tools | Import, signed webhook, five pull connectors (§4.7); validation, authenticity review that annotates |
| Enrichment | Sentiment, urgency and theme tags | Language model with few-shot exemplars; direct identifiers redacted first |
| Synthesis | Cluster signals into theme insights | Tag-based clustering with cross-signal severity; past learnings retrieved into the prompt |
| Candidates | Insights and journey-stage groups become candidates | A candidate becomes a problem only on human acceptance |
| Action | Propose actions with a risk level and an owner | Proposals on the problem record; policy checks per proposal |
| Approval | Human clearance before anything leaves the system | Interrupt; approval record bound to the verified login; blocking checks; optional four-eyes |
| Measurement | Establish whether the problem recurred | Outcome contract at approval; scheduled checkpoints; interrupted time series with an evidence grade |
| Learning | Keep reviewed conclusions with decaying confidence | Learning memory; confidence-weighted retrieval into synthesis |
| Governance | Make decisions attributable and inspectable | Policy rules, audit records with a hashed evidence pack, exports, model card, works-council pack |

Deterministic logic (validation, policy checks, audit logging, execution, outcome scoring) lives in testable code; bounded language-model reasoning (enrichment, synthesis) sits behind one interface, so the points at which the system can act are finite and auditable.

## 4.3 Non-Trivial Design Decisions

Five decisions concentrate the design contribution. Chapter 6 distinguishes their kinds: cross-signal severity (§4.3.3) and the industry profiles (§4.3.5) are implementation decisions; graduated authority (§4.3.1), perishable memory (§4.3.2) and the contracted, graded measurement of §4.4 carry the contribution. Each is implemented and tested in the current build (§4.1 dates what is deployed).

### 4.3.1 Graduated authority: every action is a draft

One rule governs how much the system may do by itself: no action reaches an external system without a recorded human approval. Approving a proposal is a LangGraph interrupt the graph cannot pass on its own, and the approval is an append-only record bound to the reviewer's verified login. Authority is graduated by the consequence class of the action rather than by a per-rule automation flag: a low-risk proposal needs one approver, a workspace can require two distinct approvers (four-eyes) for consequential actions, and a proposal that fails a blocking policy check cannot be approved until the check passes. Feedback rules are inspectable configuration that informs routing and never fires an action unattended. The design sits at a fixed, low level on the automation scale of Parasuraman, Sheridan, and Wickens (2000): the computer proposes and ranks, the human decides. The reason is a design commitment that the routing accuracy later measured (§5A.4.1) bears out: routing output is a draft (§4.7 records the first cycle's rule engine and its retirement). The gate controls false positives; it cannot recover escalation-worthy signals the enrichment ranks too low (22 of 50 under the seed labels on the production rubric, §5A.4).

### 4.3.2 Confidence decay in learnings

Each learning stores `last_validated_at` and a `half_life_days`, and a decayed confidence is computed on read by exponential decay (`base × 0.5^(age / half_life)`; `services/learning_engine.py`); learnings past their half-life are flagged stale. This treats organisational memory as perishable (Argote, 2013; Walsh & Ungson, 1991), and a practitioner can see why a learning has been down-weighted. Decay-based machine memory has prior art (Zhong et al., 2024); the contribution claimed, within the literature reviewed (§2.7), is its application to action-outcome learnings surfaced under governance into future decisions.

Learnings persist in a dedicated store (`services/learning_store.py`) and are retrieved into synthesis. Small probes (§5A.7) show that retrieval can alter recommendations on GLM-5.2; the effect did not reproduce on the production default. The probes varied whether a learning was retrieved, not its age, and ran on simulation-authored learnings over simulated outcome data, a condition the retrieval gate no longer admits (since August 2026 only conclusions a person recorded are retrieval-eligible). Decay is an implemented design hypothesis whose benefit is untested (§6.4).

### 4.3.3 Cross-signal severity scoring

Synthesis computes a composite severity from the strongest member urgency, the volume of corroborating signals and the proportion of negative sentiment, by analogy with data-fusion conflict resolution (Bleiholder & Naumann, 2008) and collective anomalies (Chandola, Banerjee, & Kumar, 2009): severity emerges from the set, countering both single-complaint over-escalation and the dilution of widespread low-urgency issues. Because the composite is volume-dominated, a corroboration floor on the emerging-candidate scorer (`services/emerging.py`) requires at least two sources or three identified customers before a candidate is labelled "action"; otherwise it is held at "watch" with a stated reason. Near-duplicate detection at import (token-set Jaccard at or above 0.85, `services/signals.py`) annotates suspected duplicates and never drops a signal, since silent deduplication would trade one bias for another.

### 4.3.4 Relevant past-learnings retrieval

Learnings are ranked by lexical overlap between the theme under synthesis and each learning's topic and tags, weighted by decayed confidence, and the top-ranked eligible learnings enter the synthesis prompt and the problem's learning panel: information retrieval (Salton & McGill, 1983) framed as case-based reasoning (Kolodner, 1993) and knowledge reuse (Markus, 2001). The ranker is a deterministic token-overlap scorer; semantic ranking is the designed upgrade path. A curated few-shot exemplar store (`services/exemplar_store.py`) teaches the enrichment model the label vocabulary and urgency rubric as prompt text; §5A.7 reports its paired A/B on the in-repo golden set as a contemporaneous record (Appendix G.4).

### 4.3.5 Per-industry scoring profiles

A triage system tuned for one sector mis-ranks another. Rather than fine-tune per sector, the platform applies per-industry weight profiles over its impact scoring (`domain/industry_profiles.py`): four named profiles (fintech, food delivery, B2B manufacturing, SaaS) override only the weights that differ from the default and are selected per workspace and editable. The cross-industry real-data run of 6 September 2026 (Appendix G.6) shows sector vocabularies emerging from enrichment without per-sector configuration, a demonstration that supports the premise that sectors differ; the weight profiles themselves have not been evaluated in any condition (§6.4). This is adaptation by configuration (De Haan, Verhoef, & Wiesel, 2015); its cost is that profiles are authored priors that can embed sector stereotypes, with inspectability as the safeguard.

Under the same stance the taxonomy is adaptive: a daily governance pass (`apply_taxonomy_governance`) promotes discovered candidates whose confidence reaches 0.80, merges near-duplicates at cosine similarity 0.95 and decays nodes that stop matching, while uploaded taxonomy is never changed automatically and merge, split and lock are human routes (§4.5). Its behaviour over time has not been evaluated.

## 4.4 Responsible AI Controls

Responsible AI is a property of the loop rather than a module (Figure 4.4). The artifact is not a high-risk system (§4.9), so a control described against a Chapter III article of the EU AI Act is a voluntary commitment; the obligations that apply are Articles 4 and 5, with Article 50(2) potentially engaged (Appendix B; the DPIA template is Appendix C).

![Figure 4.4: Signal lifecycle as a sequence: enrichment with identifiers redacted, candidate acceptance, per-action human approval with policy checks, execution with an audit record, scheduled measurement against the outcome contract, and a reviewer's learning conclusion entering the memory.](../diagrams/rendered/05_sequence_hitl.png)

- **Human-in-the-loop approval.** Every action proposal requires an explicit human approval before anything leaves the system (§4.3.1); there is no automated path around the approval interrupt, and a blocking policy check withholds approval until it passes. An approval signs one revision of one action and the outbound content it would send: the approval record stores a snapshot of the action and of the outbound title, statement, severity and evidence (with its hash), and dispatch, whether the first push or the retry of a failed push, is authorised against that approved revision and content under the decisions still in force, so an edit to the action or the problem after approval, a rejection that revokes the approval, or a newer approval run that superseded the execution refuses the dispatch; edits and approvals on one problem are serialised, and a dispatch is claimed at the store before the connector is called, so two workers cannot send it twice. Because awareness alone is unlikely to counter automation bias (Laux & Ruschemeier, 2025), the approver sees the evidence, the policy-check results and the model score (Siebert et al., 2023). The gate controls false positives, wrong or harmful actions reaching an external system; it cannot recover escalation-worthy signals the enrichment ranks too low (22 of 50 under the seed labels on the production rubric, §5A.4).
- **Audit trail and transparency.** Every approval and execution is an append-only workflow record naming its reviewer (a pseudonym derived from the verified login) or executor, the connector result and an audit block; every AI-derived output carries provenance metadata (model, source, stated limitations). Under Article 50 (Appendix B), paragraph 50(2) is a provider-side marking duty on synthetic text output, assessed by output and role, so CLARA as a provider of a system that drafts text is potentially engaged and machine-readable marking of exported drafts is the open item; paragraph 50(4) is not engaged for internal tickets.
- **Outcome contract and closure levels.** The contract (metric, baseline, threshold, window) is proposed at approval and can be edited or declined. Closure has three meanings the readout keeps apart: delivery (an execution record with the connector's external reference), target attainment (an instrumented reading from a closing checkpoint met the contracted threshold over its frozen interval, which issues the loop verdict) and attributed improvement (a fitted interrupted time series, §3.5.5, graded C only when the fit was obtained and never a validated causal design); the estimator refuses below its minimum data requirements (10 pre-days, 5 post-days). Each contract is versioned: every amendment creates a new revision with author, time and note, a reading already recorded is scored under the revision in force when it was taken, and pending checkpoints carry the terms frozen when they were scheduled and are re-planned explicitly when the contract is amended. The measurement clock starts at dispatch, when the approved action leaves the system (at approval only for a draft-only deliverable, which the readout labels), or at a human-recorded implementation instant that supersedes the earlier checkpoints; a failed push starts no clock.
- **Attributable, tamper-evident approvals.** Reviewer identity is bound to the verified login (a JWT-derived pseudonym) rather than self-asserted in the request body. Every evidence pack carries a canonical sha256 content hash stamped on the approval record at decision time, so whether the pack an approver saw has since changed is provable. The approval record carries a snapshot of the action revision it signs and, in the current build, names the execution it created and the outbound content it authorises, with that content's hash.
- **Measurement grading and provenance.** The measurement design (scheduled checkpoints, the estimator and its refusal rule, the A–E evidence grade, instrumented-versus-manual provenance, the worsening-threshold check) is specified in §3.5.5 and mapped to code in Appendix D. Every readout grades the design actually realised, so a refused fit reads D and never C; manual values are labelled "unverified manual observation" with provenance the API route stamps so a client cannot spoof it.
- **Model sovereignty.** A fail-closed residency gate (`CLARA_AI_REQUIRE_EU=1`) refuses a provider the residency classifier cannot place in the EU or on the deployer's host; the gateway behind the GLM-5.2 figures is classified "unknown" by it, and no self-hosted model has been run on either gold set (§6.4).
- **Trust calibration.** The label "confidence" was renamed "model score", an uncalibrated heuristic, because model confidence is typically miscalibrated (Guo, Pleiss, Sun, & Weinberger, 2017) and a heuristic presented as a probability invites miscalibrated trust (Lee & See, 2004).
- **Worker-facing transparency.** An opt-in works-council pack supports a works agreement under the German Works Constitution Act, §87(1) no. 6, rather than obviating one, and a CI test fails if any non-admin endpoint leaks a person-capable field.

The ingestion authenticity review annotates provenance, exact duplication, volume bursts and batch-level uniformity for a human decision; what it declines to do was decided by measurement. Two candidate detectors of machine-written reviews were measured on 6,740 real feedback texts: a stylometric detector at 81% recall flagged 15.0% of genuine reviews, skewed toward angrier ones (mean 2.21 stars against 2.70), with a flag rate varying 3.0× across languages; a Binoculars-style cross-perplexity detector (Hans et al., 2024) scored AUROC 0.326 on the English stratum, worse than chance. Authorship attribution is therefore not implemented; the shipped gate flags 0.86% of 6,154 genuine reviews (all exact duplicates) and abstains below ten words, 25.1% of those reviews and 23% of all 6,740 texts. It has been wired into every ingestion path since 21 August 2026 (Appendix G.8).

## 4.5 Data Model

Figure 4.5 draws the data model; Appendix E extracts its DDL from the migrations. Domain aggregates are stored as one JSONB document per row, validated on every read and write by the pydantic model that is the aggregate's schema, and every table carries a leading workspace key with row-level security enabled and forced.

![Figure 4.5: Data model (entity-relationship) of the current build (§4.1 dates what is deployed). The outcome contract (DP1) lives on the problem record; approvals, executions, measurements, guardrails and learning conclusions are typed workflow records under the problem; reviewed conclusions are copied into the learning memory (DP2), whose confidence decays on read; taxonomy nodes self-reference for the three-level hierarchy.](../diagrams/rendered/06_data_model_er.png)

Signals are the ingested feedback with the enrichment written back by triage; only the identifier and the text are required. A problem is what a person accepted: evidence, affected cohort, impact factors, action proposals with risk levels and approval state, policy-check results and the outcome contract. Everything the loop then does to a problem is a typed workflow record (approval, execution, outcome measurement with its instrumented-or-manual provenance, guardrail measurement, closure, learning conclusion), named for its reviewer or executor. Measurement plans are the scheduled checkpoints (T+7, window, follow-up), each carrying its clock origin, the contract terms frozen at scheduling and the fixed observation interval it reads. Learnings are reviewed conclusions with base confidence, half-life and last validation date.

The taxonomy is a three-level structure in `taxonomy_nodes`, the one table with an embedding column (§4.3.5). The gold-set evaluation (Chapter 5) scores the enrichment pipeline's journey-stage and owner assignments against the datasets' own seed labels, not against this structure; theme carries seed labels but no metric.

## 4.6 Evaluation Instrumentation

The platform records a telemetry stream of named events for the task-based practitioner evaluation designed in Chapter 3 and not conducted at the time of writing (§5B.1): candidate acceptance, approval decisions (`approval_recorded`), executions, checkpoints and loop verdicts, with no person identifier on any row; time-to-action would be derived offline from the approval and transition timestamps. The offline gold-set harness (§5A) scores two predictors: a generic triage prompt (`predict_llm.py`) and the artifact's own `enrich_signals` stage with its prompt, exemplars and sanitiser (`predict_llm_production.py`, §5A.2).

The artifact was built through build-evaluate cycles (Hevner et al., 2004; Table 4.1). Appendix G.8 dates each cycle, the repository history carries the commits, and Appendix F reports the external-review episode of July 2026, on which Chapter 5 relies for the provenance of several controls.

Table 4.1: Design cycles.

One retired design is recorded because Chapter 6 abstracts a principle from the difference. The first iteration governed action with a rule engine: matching rules were ranked by priority, ties broken by specificity, duplicates removed by action type, and an action whose rule allowed it fired without review (McDermott & Forgy, 1978; Boyer & Mili, 2011). It was retired on the design ground that an unattended rule would automate the pipeline's least reliable judgement, a judgement the routing accuracy later measured (§5A.4.1) bears out, and because an approval record is produced by the act of deciding whereas a rule set pays off only if maintained.

The platform integrates with collection and delivery tools. The action side is live: on approval with a destination connector configured, `services/action_push.py` performs the external write (Jira issue, Slack message); pushes are idempotent, a push failure is recorded on the execution rather than failing the approval, and a local draft is the fallback. Pull connectors cover five sources through official APIs (Zendesk, Trustpilot, the Apple App Store, Google Play, Google Business Profile); HubSpot is a draft destination only, and no Typeform or Intercom integration is built. A wiring defect of the same kind as the approval-interrupt defect of §4.9, triage enrichment computed in pipeline state but never persisted to the signal store, was found and fixed in the July 2026 review cycle. The contribution is a situated instantiation of a governed loop-closure design, not a finished product.

## 4.8 A Worked Example

Consider one signal from the fintech dataset of the evaluation corpus, an English paraphrase of a public review rather than a verbatim: a customer reports that the transaction history cannot be exported to CSV or Excel. The signal is enriched with negative sentiment, a transaction-export theme and medium urgency, with the paraphrased span as evidence; in synthesis it clusters with other export-related signals and the composite severity (§4.3.3) rises with corroboration. A reviewer accepts the candidate, and the problem record carries an action proposal, a backlog item routed to the investment-product owner; the proposal waits at the approval interrupt (§4.3.1). A product manager approves it, and the system proposes the outcome contract: the daily rate of export-related signals against the trailing rate over 28 observed days, a 30-day window with a T+7 early read, a 50% reduction as the target (`services/outcome_engine.py`).

The system executes by creating the Jira item with the paraphrased evidence attached (an external write when a connector is configured, a local draft otherwise) and writes the approval and execution records; the clock starts at dispatch. An implementation date a reviewer later records is an attestation the system does not verify, and the fixed observation window runs from that instant. The window and follow-up checkpoints read the frozen interval, with an interrupted-time-series estimate beside the reading that is refused below its minimum data requirements; a reading that met the target records target attainment, graded D like every instrumented checkpoint reading (E if manual), with attribution stated as not established. The reviewer's qualified conclusion enters the memory with the default half-life (180 days) and is retrieved, weighted by decayed confidence, when a similar gap is next synthesised.

Of these steps, the following have been executed. The seeded demonstration workspace (Figures 4.6 and 4.7, July 2026; fixtures, no evaluation dataset) shows a problem record, its proposed actions, the approval gate and the Outcome Board before any checkpoint has fallen due. The path from imported signals through acceptance, approval, dispatch, checkpoint reading and loop verdict is executed by the regression suite on simulated signal series (`test_loop_closure.py`), and the estimator has run on simulated outcome data only. The release, the implementation date and the reduction are illustrative and have not been observed on any dataset. Chapter 5 scores sentiment against star-rating proxies and risk, journey stage and owner against seed labels; theme and action carry labels but no metric.

Creating the Jira item records operational delivery. If the feature is subsequently released, a reviewer records its implementation date and the observation window runs from that intervention. A qualifying checkpoint can show that complaint inflow met the contracted target. Without a defensible causal design, the learning records an observed reduction after release and its limitations, rather than asserting that the feature caused the reduction.

## 4.9 Risk Classification, Threat Model, and Failure Modes

EU AI Act risk tier. A structured reading of Annex III places the artifact outside the high-risk category: its outputs are internal operational routing with human approval for consequential actions, and it makes no decision about identified individuals' access to services, credit, employment or education. A churn-risk segment is not a vulnerable group in the sense of Article 5(1)(b), yet the design adopts a stricter policy constraint: no manipulative or exploitative outreach at all, enforced by a blocking policy check on customer-facing action classes. The classification is documented in Appendix B.

Threat model. The capability to take action is also the principal risk surface.

| Threat | Mitigation in the design | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| Manipulative or exploitative outreach | Blocking policy checks on customer-facing action classes; approval for every action | A dark-pattern enforcement gate is not implemented |
| Model error in enrichment leading to mis-routing | Human approval, audit trail, the model score labelled an uncalibrated heuristic | The gate controls false positives but cannot recover escalation-worthy signals ranked too low (22 of 50 under the seed labels on the production rubric) |
| Personal data reaching the model | Direct identifiers redacted before enrichment and synthesis; self-hostable model (not exercised, §4.4) | Pattern-based redaction: a name in running text is not caught |
| Machine-generated or coordinated feedback | Authenticity review (§4.4) annotated for a human decision; the corroboration floor | A varied batch of machine-written text with no duplication was not detected by either detector measured in §4.4 |

Review, then harden. The code review of 2 July 2026 found the approval-interrupt defect: the interrupt was never resumed in production, so the action, measure and learn stages were unreachable until the fix, and the audit trail and telemetry made the failure visible. Appendix G.8 records the review and the hardening passes.

**Failure modes.** When measurement is inconclusive (no clear movement within the window, or a series too sparse to fit), the problem is not marked resolved: the readout says so, the evidence grade says what kind of evidence exists, and the loop verdict stays open rather than silently closing. Closure is asserted only on evidence: a loop-closure verdict (*loop closed* or *no improvement observed*; the platform does not say that the fix did not land, because an uncontrolled read cannot attribute the absence of movement to the action) is certified only by an instrumented reading that a closing checkpoint (the window read or the follow-up read) produced over its fixed observation interval under the terms frozen when it was scheduled, and the reading itself carries that binding (checkpoint, plan, execution, the contract terms it was scored under and the interval it covers); an early T+7 read or a manual reading never certifies, whatever the checkpoint ledger says, and a reading whose clock ran from approval rather than dispatch or implementation carries the caveat that it may predate the fix. Competing proposals are decided by the reviewer at the approval step, and stale learnings are down-weighted by decay rather than silently trusted: the system is designed to fail visibly rather than claim an unwarranted closure.
