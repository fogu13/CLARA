# Appendix G: Provenance and Revision Log

The chapters describe the artifact and the evaluation in their final state. This appendix holds the dated provenance they cross-reference: what was run when, what was lost, what was attested rather than observed, and what changed between drafts. The claim ledger `thesis/CLAIM_LEDGER.md` carries each entry's commit; the response documents in `docs/reviews/` and `thesis/review-2026-09-04-*.md` record the external review rounds that prompted most corrections.

## G.1 The seed labels and the second risk rating (§3.7)

The three datasets, including the collection of the public pages, the paraphrases, the de-identification and the seed labels, were produced in a ChatGPT research session on 21 June 2026 and used as delivered. The same process later produced three further datasets the harness can load but the thesis does not use; their loader notes describe their seed labels as assistant-drafted and pending review. The datasets' own READMEs describe the seed labels as included for testing and not as validated gold-standard annotations.

The second rating of the risk seeds is a 40-item sample drawn on 6 September 2026 (`evaluation/kappa_sample.py`, seed 20260905, from the 106 risk-labelled Trade Republic and Henkel signals, seed labels withheld from the file). What is observed and what is attested are kept apart. What is observed in the repository: the rubric (`kappa_labelling_instructions.md`) was committed at 16:43 UTC, the filled sample and its κ file (`kappa_sample.csv`, `kappa_risk.json`) together at 17:12 UTC, under the author's git identity; nothing committed identifies a rater, a hand-over or a return time; the rubric estimates 30 to 45 minutes for the task. What the external review of 13 September 2026 reports: an AI-assistant task record titled "Fill blind risk labels" (id `01a077a7-b101-78c0-accc-97f813223a23`) whose 40 labels are identical to the committed file, an observation the repository can neither confirm nor refute because no such record is committed. What is attested: the owner stated on 12 September 2026 that "another rater" produced the labels. The two are not reconciled, so every chapter reports the rating as second-rating agreement with independent human provenance unverified, never as a human check; the statistic itself (κ = 0.55 unweighted, 0.38–0.72; 0.70 linear-weighted, 0.56–0.82; 27 of 40 exact; 38 of 40 within one level) is reproducible from the committed files, which are unchanged. The full record, and the rule for any further rating (a new draw, a new commit, the rater's role, hand-over and return dates and an AI-access statement), is `evaluation/results/PROVENANCE_kappa.md`. The annotation kit that implements that rule for the whole corpus is `evaluation/annotation_kit.py` (§6.5).

## G.2 The in-repo golden set: authoring, audit and the run ledger (§5A.7)

The golden set was authored in the repository during development, in the same AI-assisted sessions that produced the code: the English items in early July 2026 without a recorded audit, the German items on 17 July and the boundary items on 18 July, the later strata audited by two independent AI reviewer agents. Neither the golden set nor the seed labels is human-labelled; they were produced independently of each other, the golden set is a development and validation benchmark co-developed with the artifact rather than independent confirmation, and the star rating is the one human-origin reference between them.

The per-item run ledger (`history.jsonl`) is excluded from the repository, and the copy holding the July runs was lost when the development machine was retired. What survives of July is four committed snapshots of `published_metrics.json` (two on 17 July, two on 18 July) and the results in their commit messages; the 3 July run and the 3 July learning-probe figure (G.6) rest on the author's contemporaneous record only.

## G.3 The held-out split: history and status (§3.5.3, §5A.7)

The split entered the repository with the set on 17 July 2026 at n = 80 (held-out 18 EN / 6 DE), took its current membership (23 EN / 7 DE) on 18 July 2026 when the set grew to 100 and the German gold labels were completed, and has been scored in the five published runs since; unpublished runs are not recoverable (G.2). The pipeline changed after the split was frozen (closed-set routing behind a flag, 8 August; measurement and audit changes, 2 September; identifier redaction at the model boundary, 5 September), so the 6 September run scores a pipeline that post-dates the split. The documented exemplar changes (German exemplars, 17 July; the churn-musing exemplar, 18 July) are the tuning the split was meant to guard; the set has not changed since 18 July.

Two facts accompany the split's numbers wherever they appear. The harness's safety veto (the hallucination guard) is pooled over every scored item, held-out included, so the split is a safety-validation set that takes part in selection while the accuracy decision is made without it. And its figures are held-out accuracies of the exemplars-on configuration, not a paired exemplars-off-versus-on effect; a per-split paired effect was not available at the time of writing.

## G.4 The golden set's run trajectory and the urgency exemplar effect (§3.5.1, §3.5.3, §4.3.4, §5A.7)

The set began as 60 English cases, grew in July 2026 to 80 through 20 authored German items (`eval-061` to `eval-080`) and reached 100 (72 EN / 28 DE) when the German stratum was completed to the English items' two-tag rubric. Two independent skeptic agents re-examined every German label against the set's rules and confirmed all but one (`eval-078`, urgency low to medium).

The chapters report the 6 September 2026 rows only; the trajectory is kept because it contains a negative result that was not re-rolled. The July accuracies rest on the committed snapshots (G.2); the 3 July run, every exemplar A/B effect and the production-default row rest on unversioned run records, cited as contemporaneous record, per-item record not preserved.

| Run (2026) | n (EN/DE) | Sentiment | Urgency | Tag F1 (fuzzy) | In-run exemplar A/B (tag set; urgency) |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 3 July | 60 (60/0) | — | — | — | underpowered on both |
| 17 July, first snapshot (German items added) | 80 (60/20) | 97.5% | 85.0% | 74.2% | significant; not significant |
| 17 July, second snapshot (two German exemplars added) | 80 (60/20) | 97.5% | 86.3% | 80.2% | significant; not significant (not claimed) |
| 18 July, later of two same-day snapshots (churn-musing exemplar added) | 100 (72/28) | 97% | 90% | 82.7% | not re-tested; significant, stable in three same-day re-runs |
| 6 September, published | 100 (72/28) | 99% (0.95–1.00); held-out 96.7% (n = 30) | 91% (0.84–0.95); held-out 93.3% | 78.7% | 9% → 25%, p = 0.002; 78% → 91%, p < 0.001 |
| 6 September, production default (Mistral Small 4), not published | 100 (72/28) | 95%; held-out 90.0% | 82%; held-out 80.0% | — | 2% → 16%, p = 0.001; 82% → 82% |

The one eval-improve iteration in the table is the German gap: the 17 July baseline scored no German exact-tag-set agreement because the few-shot exemplars were English-only, and two German exemplars written fresh lifted it to a significant in-run effect and narrowed the DE/EN urgency gap without regressing English. The headline figures pool the optimisation half (n = 70) and the held-out half (23 EN / 7 DE), reported separately since 6 September (G.3).

The in-run paired A/B on urgency was read at n = 60, 80 and 100 and first crossed the 0.05 line at n = 100, after the churn-musing calibration exemplar had been added. Testing one hypothesis at successive sample sizes and claiming at the first crossing is sequential testing without alpha spending, so the claim is held as exploratory in the statistical sense. The three same-day re-runs bound model noise but repeat no test over new items, and the held-out split supplies no confirmatory read (G.3); neither substitutes for alpha control. On 6 September the urgency effect appears on GLM-5.2 and not on the production default, while the tag-vocabulary effect appears on both.

Publication is a committed step, not a deployment: `GET /model-card/metrics` renders whatever `published_metrics.json` snapshot the served build carries, and the build serving production on 15 September 2026 (G.8) still carried the 18 July snapshot.

## G.5 The generic-prompt run and a harness defect (§5A.2)

The contextual LLM path was run against the 188-signal corpus on 4 August 2026, enriching 188 of 188 signals. An earlier attempt had failed silently: the gateway rejected the client library's default User-Agent header with an HTTP 403, and the script exited with status 0 after writing an empty results file. The defect was treated as a finding about the harness; the fix (a User-Agent header and a hard failure on an empty result set) is committed alongside the results (§4.9).

## G.6 The production-stage runs, their repeats and the other 6 September runs (§5A.4.2, §5A.7)

The artifact's enrichment stage was run on the corpus on 5 September 2026, twice, with scoring rules fixed before either run (`predictions_llm_production.meta.json`). The GLM-5.2 run used batches of ten rather than the production default of twenty-five because a twenty-five-item batch exceeded the client's read timeout; it scored 188 of 188 in 37 minutes, the Mistral Small 4 run 188 of 188 in under two minutes at the production batch size. The executed contrast between the 4 August generic run and the 5 September production run on GLM-5.2 is a configuration contrast, prompt/pipeline × run date, not an isolated pipeline effect (G.7). The planned matched rerun (`evaluation/run_matrix.py`, not executed at the time of writing) is a prompt-and-pipeline configuration × model design, with the generic prompt scored per item and the production stage batched at the requested size.

The production default was run three more times under the identical configuration (`results_mistral-small-2603_run1` to `_run3`) and the generic prompt on GLM-5.2 three more times on 6 September (`results_glm_generic_run1` to `_run3`; the 4 August file remains the reported run). The four-run figures are read from the `compare_runs_*.csv` files in the Mistral folder (`mistral_run0` to `mistral_run3`) and at the top level of `evaluation/results/`; `summary.json` carries paired tests for the GLM-5.2 runs only and no Mistral pair.

Run-to-run stability (raw per-run McNemar values). The production default is close to deterministic at temperature 0: identical sentiment labels on 99.3–100% of the 153 star-rated signals and identical urgency labels on 97.2–100% of the 106 risk-labelled ones. The generic prompt on GLM-5.2 agrees with itself on 97.4–99.3% of sentiment labels, its lead over TF-IDF + logistic regression holding in every run (b = 7 throughout, c = 19 or 20; raw p = 0.019–0.029), but on only 85.8–94.3% of risk labels, split by date: the three September runs agree with one another on 93.4–94.3% and with the August run on 85.8–86.8%, score 0.65–0.66 against its 0.72, and label 20 or 21 of the 27 gold-critical signals *high* against 17 in August (`risk_llm_confusion.csv`). The production stage's risk deficit holds against all four generic runs (raw p < 0.001 in each pairing).

The cross-industry real-data run was re-executed on 6 September 2026 on the production default (`real_data_2026-09-06_mistral-small-2603.json`). Under its lenient rule (*mixed* counts as correct for one-to-three-star items) it agrees with the star rating on 128 of 144 polar-rated signals (89%), so it is not comparable to §5A.3; it shows mean urgency falling monotonically with star rating in all three datasets and sector vocabularies emerging without per-sector configuration (§4.3.5).

The learning-loop probe (§5A.7; DP2) measured the share of a stored learning's remedy appearing in a new recommendation: 21% → 71% on 3 July 2026 on GLM-5.2, contemporaneous record only. The 6 September re-run shows adoption on GLM-5.2 (three of three themes) and not on the production default (one of four): model-specific and, at four themes, fragile. The probe varied whether a learning was retrieved, not its age, so it measures retrieval, not perishability (§6.4). Three further scripts had not been run at the time of writing: the matched rerun, an embedding-plus-logistic-regression baseline (`evaluation/predict_embedding.py`) and the equity comparison between predictors (`evaluation/equity_gap_bootstrap.py`).

## G.7 Corrections between drafts

- **Routing macro-F1 on supported classes** (§5A.4.1). An earlier draft's lower figures were an averaging artefact; corrected, the figures are 0.55 and 0.42.
- **Cross-language classification** (§5A.5). An aggregate "German versus English" comparison was retracted because language is confounded with sector and the texts are English paraphrases; only escalation recall per source stratum is reported, descriptively.
- **The weighted-κ interval** (§3.7). The manuscript printed a lower upper bound than the committed file, which rounds it to 0.82.
- **The second rating's status** (§3.7, G.1). Between 12 and 13 September 2026 the rating was described as a blind human rating on the owner's attestation; since the 13 September review it is second-rating agreement with independent human provenance unverified.
- **The survey script's reporting tiers** (§5B.3). Until 13 September 2026 `survey_analysis.py` wrote a verdict at n = 10 while the pre-registered rule issues verdicts from n = 40; the script now enforces the rule, and its CONFIRMED label is reported as "supported in this sample".
- **The evidence grader** (§3.5.5). Until 13 September 2026 the grade was inferred from substrings of the method name; it is now read from an explicit table and grades the design as realised.
- **The "pipeline effect"** (§5A.4.2). The 4 August generic run and the 5 September production run on GLM-5.2 were described as differing only in the pipeline; because the generic prompt's September repeats agree with its August run on only 85.8–86.8% of risk labels (G.6), the pair is a configuration contrast, prompt/pipeline × run date.
- **Multiple comparisons** (§5A.3, §5A.6). Unadjusted until 13 September 2026, then Holm-adjusted within two families of six comparisons per task; the 17 September revision replaced that with one retrospective family per task of the ten unique comparisons among the five predictors (`evaluation/multiple_comparisons.py`, `results/paired_tests_holm.csv`); §5A.6 states the one status that changed.
- **Scope and evidence status** (17 September 2026). The practitioner study had not run; its protocol, instruments and pre-registered tiers are retained (§5B; Appendix A), and two unbound working files were added: `thesis/instruments/survey_distribution.md` and `thesis/instruments/followup_optin.md`.

## G.8 Dated design cycles that the chapters describe in the present tense

- **2 July 2026.** A structured code review (`docs/archive/code-review-2026-07-02.md`) confirmed high-severity defects (unauthenticated read endpoints, an authentication guard enforced only client-side, row-level security declared but not enforced because the API connected as the table owner) and found the approval-interrupt defect that had silently disabled the outcome loop in production (§4.7, §4.9); a hardening pass followed within days (authentication on read endpoints, a per-workspace rate limiter, SSRF guards, RLS backfilled). Mitigations that existed on paper held only after adversarial verification; the platform is hardened for the evaluation context, not warranted production-multi-tenant.
- **16–17 July 2026.** Two independent LLM-based strategic reviews of the deployed artifact, checked against the codebase and the live deployment before being acted on (Appendix F reports the episode and its claim-by-claim tally; a naturalistic ex-post input, no methodological novelty claimed). The cycle added the A–E evidence grade on every outcome readout, server-side measurement provenance, the hashed evidence pack, reviewer identity bound to the verified login, the opt-in four-eyes mode and the published per-language metrics snapshot (§4.4); hardened the session perimeter (cookie sessions, TOTP, encrypted connector secrets); gave the MCP server strictly read-only tools behind revocable, role-scoped API keys; gave the German works-council pack a code-verifiable no-individual-performance-monitoring attestation with its residual limits stated. The ITS bias controls (§3.5.5) date from the same month.
- **8 August 2026.** Closed-set routing behind a flag; only human-recorded conclusions retrieval-eligible (§4.3.2).
- **21 August 2026.** The ingestion authenticity review wired into every ingestion path (294c7b7; §4.4).
- **2–5 September 2026.** Readiness route, measurement and audit changes, identifier redaction at the model boundary; the production-stage runs (G.6).
- **12–13 September 2026.** Two external review rounds and their responses (`docs/reviews/`): measurement clock origins and bound readings (migration 016); frozen contract terms, fixed observation intervals and explicit re-planning (migration 017); the outbound content bound to the approval and the store-level dispatch claim (§4.4); the explicit grade table; the survey tiers; the provenance wording of G.1. ## G.9 The competitor claim check (§6.3)

The comparison with Enterpret in §6.3 rests on an assistant-led claim check run in July 2026 over public material, mostly Enterpret's own pages, with three-vote adversarial checking of each testable claim. Documented in the sources reviewed: a large connector catalogue, an adaptive taxonomy with drift and duplicate detection, conversational querying, an MCP server, and bidirectional ticket routing to Jira, Linear and Zendesk. Contradicted by the sources: the autonomy claims most relevant to this thesis, since its agents require human approval and its taxonomy proposes rather than deploys changes. Its outcome measurement is not documented in the sources reviewed. The exercise reads vendor documentation, not the products.
