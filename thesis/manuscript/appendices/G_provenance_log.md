# Appendix G: Provenance and Revision Log

The chapters describe the artifact and the evaluation in their final state. This appendix holds the dated provenance the chapters cross-reference: what was run when, what was lost, what was attested rather than observed, and what changed between drafts. Every entry also appears, with the commit that carries each artefact, in the claim ledger `thesis/CLAIM_LEDGER.md`; the response documents in `docs/reviews/` record the three external review rounds (4, 12 and 13 September 2026) that prompted most of the corrections.

## G.1 The seed labels and the second risk rating (§3.7)

The three datasets, including the collection of the public pages, the paraphrases, the de-identification and the seed labels, were produced in a ChatGPT research session on 21 June 2026 and used as delivered. The same process later produced three further datasets the harness can load but the thesis does not use; their loader notes describe their seed labels as assistant-drafted and pending review. The datasets' own READMEs describe the seed labels as included for testing and not as validated gold-standard annotations.

The second rating of the risk seeds is a 40-item sample drawn on 6 September 2026 (`evaluation/kappa_sample.py`, seed 20260905, from the 106 risk-labelled Trade Republic and Henkel signals, seed labels withheld from the file). What is observed in the repository: the rubric (`kappa_labelling_instructions.md`) was committed at 16:43 UTC, the filled sample and its κ file (`kappa_sample.csv`, `kappa_risk.json`) together at 17:12 UTC, under the author's git identity; nothing committed identifies a rater, a hand-over or a return time; the rubric estimates 30 to 45 minutes for the task. What the external review of 13 September 2026 reports: an AI-assistant task record titled "Fill blind risk labels" (id `01a077a7-b101-78c0-accc-97f813223a23`) whose 40 labels are identical to the committed file, an observation the repository can neither confirm nor refute because no such record is committed. What is attested: the owner stated on 12 September 2026 that "another rater" produced the labels. The two are not reconciled, so every chapter reports the rating as second-rating agreement with independent human provenance unverified, never as a human check; the statistic itself (κ = 0.55 unweighted, 0.38–0.72; 0.70 linear-weighted, 0.56–0.82; 27 of 40 exact; 38 of 40 on escalate-or-not) is reproducible from the committed files, which are unchanged. The full record, and the rule for any further rating (a new draw, a new commit, the rater's role, hand-over and return dates and an AI-access statement), is `evaluation/results/PROVENANCE_kappa.md`. The annotation kit that implements that rule for the whole corpus is `evaluation/annotation_kit.py` (§6.5).

## G.2 The in-repo golden set: authoring, audit and the run ledger (§5A.7)

The golden set was authored in the repository during development, in the same sessions with the AI coding assistant that produced the code: the sixty English items in early July 2026 without a recorded audit, the twenty German items on 17 July and the twenty boundary items on 18 July, each stratum audited by two independent AI reviewer agents (19 of 20 and 18 of 20 confirmed; the run note in `published_metrics.json` records the German audit). Neither the golden set nor the seed labels is human-labelled; they are independent of each other and of the artifact, and the star rating is the one human-origin reference between them.

The per-item run ledger (`history.jsonl`) is deliberately excluded from the repository. The copy that held the July runs was not preserved when the development machine was retired, so what survives of July is the four committed snapshots of `published_metrics.json` (17 July 07:24 and 15:22 UTC; 18 July 12:25 and 14:28 UTC) with the run results recorded in their commit messages. The 3 July row of the §5A.7 table and the learning-probe figures of §5A.7 disclosure (v) rest on the author's contemporaneous record only. The September runs are in the repository: the 6 September `published_metrics.json` and the dated files in the thesis results folders.

## G.3 The held-out split: history and status (§3.5.3, §5A.7)

The split entered the repository with the set on 17 July 2026 at n = 80 (held-out 18 EN / 6 DE), took its current membership (23 EN / 7 DE) on 18 July 2026 when the set grew to 100 and the German gold labels were completed, and was scored in the five published runs since (two on 17 July on the 80-item split, two on 18 July and one on 6 September on the 100-item split); the number of unpublished runs is not recoverable, because the run ledger and reports are not versioned. Its per-item failures were printed to the operator on each run until 6 September 2026, when the harness began writing them to a sidecar that is opened only with an explicit flag. The enrichment pipeline changed after the split was frozen (closed-set routing behind a flag on 8 August; measurement and audit changes on 2 September; identifier redaction at the model boundary on 5 September 2026), so the 6 September run scores a pipeline that post-dates the split. The documented exemplar changes (the German exemplars of 17 July and the churn-musing exemplar of 18 July) are the tuning the split was meant to guard; the golden set itself has not changed since 18 July.

Two facts about the split's role are stated wherever its numbers appear. The harness's safety veto (the hallucination guard that can reject a candidate change) is pooled over every scored item, held-out items included, so the split is a safety-validation set that takes part in selection, while the accuracy decision is made without it. And the figures reported for it are held-out accuracies of the exemplars-on configuration, not a paired exemplars-off-versus-on effect on the held-out items; the per-split paired effect is computed by the updated harness from its next run onward and was not available at the time of writing.

## G.4 The urgency exemplar effect: sequential testing (§3.5.3, §4.3.4)

The in-run paired A/B on urgency read p ≈ 1.0 at n = 60 (3 July), p = 0.219 at n = 80 (17 July, not claimed), and p = 0.039 at n = 100 (18 July) after a churn-musing calibration exemplar had been added between the n = 80 and n = 100 runs; the "three runs" behind the 18 July figure are same-day re-runs of identical items, which bound model noise (GLM-5.2 is non-deterministic at temperature 0) and are not replication over items. Testing the same hypothesis at successive sample sizes and claiming at the first crossing is sequential testing without alpha spending, so the claim is held as exploratory. The 6 September run reproduces the in-run effect, pooled over both halves, on GLM-5.2 (78% → 91%, thirteen items gained and none lost, p < 0.001) and finds none on the production default (82% → 82%), so the urgency effect is model-specific while the tag-vocabulary effect is not (p = 0.002 and p = 0.001 on the two models).

## G.5 The generic-prompt run and a harness defect (§5A.2)

The contextual LLM path was run against the 188-signal corpus on 4 August 2026, enriching 188 of 188 signals. An earlier execution attempt had failed silently: the model gateway rejected the client library's default User-Agent header with an HTTP 403, and the script exited with status 0 after writing an empty results file, so a green exit code stood in for a run that had produced nothing. The defect was treated as a finding about the harness rather than quietly rerun; the fix (a User-Agent header, and a hard failure on an empty result set) is committed alongside the results. A harness that can report success on no output is the failure mode §4.9 warns against: declared checks are not enforced checks until they fail loudly.

## G.6 The production-stage runs and their repeats (§5A.4.2)

The artifact's own enrichment stage was run on the corpus on 5 September 2026, twice, under scoring rules fixed before either run and recorded in each run's metadata file (a *mixed* prediction maps to *neutral* against the three-class star gold, with a drop-*mixed* sensitivity beside it; the urgency label is scored as risk unchanged). The GLM-5.2 run used batches of ten signals rather than the production default of twenty-five, because a twenty-five-item batch through that model's gateway exceeded the client's read timeout: the first attempt lost 75 of 188 signals to timeouts and was discarded, and the retained run scored 188 of 188 in 37 minutes. The Mistral Small 4 run used the production batch size and scored 188 of 188 in under two minutes. Wall-clock time through each provider's gateway is reported as observed, not as a controlled latency benchmark.

The production default was then run three more times under the identical configuration (`results_mistral-small-2603_run1` to `_run3`; wall-clock 92 to 407 seconds across the four runs), and the generic prompt on GLM-5.2 three more times on 6 September (`results_glm_generic_run1` to `_run3`; the 4 August file remains the reported run). The four-run figures of §5A.4.2 are read from `compare_runs_accuracy.csv`, `compare_runs_agreement.csv` and `compare_runs_pairs.csv` in the Mistral folder (runs `mistral_run0` to `mistral_run3`) and at the top level of `evaluation/results/` (the GLM-5.2 generic repeats). `evaluation/results/summary.json` carries paired tests for the GLM-5.2 runs only and no Mistral pair, so the deployment-configuration pair is read from the CSV alone. The production stage on GLM-5.2 was run once; it is not the deployed configuration. A matched rerun of both prompts on both models on one day at one batch size, the design that would isolate the model effect, is scripted (`evaluation/run_matrix.py`) and had not been run at the time of writing.

## G.7 Corrections between drafts

- **Routing macro-F1 on supported classes** (§5A.4.1). An earlier draft reported 0.28 for journey stage and 0.18 for owner; that was an averaging artefact of the harness, which admitted every out-of-set prediction on the restricted items as a phantom class with F1 = 0. The harness is corrected and the figures are 0.55 and 0.42.
- **Cross-language classification** (§5A.5). An earlier aggregate "German versus English" comparison was retracted rather than defended, because language is confounded with sector and the texts are English paraphrases; only escalation recall per source stratum is reported.
- **The weighted-κ interval** (§3.7). The manuscript printed an upper bound of 0.81; the committed file rounds it to 0.82.
- **The second rating's status** (§3.7, G.1). Between 12 and 13 September 2026 the rating was described as a blind human rating on the owner's attestation; since the 13 September review it is described as second-rating agreement with independent human provenance unverified.
- **The survey script's reporting tiers** (§5B.1). Until 13 September 2026 `survey_analysis.py` wrote a verdict from n = 10 while the pre-registered rule issues verdicts from n = 40; the script now enforces the rule (counts only below n = 10, descriptive from 10 to 39, verdicts from 40 on each hypothesis's own usable denominator).
- **The evidence grader** (§3.5.5). Until 13 September 2026 the grade was inferred from substrings of the method name, so a contract labelled `uncontrolled_before_after` read A; the grade is now read from an explicit table and grades the design as realised (A and B are reserved and read D; C only with a fitted interrupted time series).
- **The "pipeline effect"** (§5A.4.2). The pair of the 4 August generic run and the 5 September production run on GLM-5.2 was described as differing only in the pipeline; because the generic prompt's own September repeats disagree with its August run on 14% of risk labels, it is described as a configuration contrast, prompt and pipeline × run date.
- **Multiple comparisons** (§5A.3, §5A.6). The paired tests were reported unadjusted until this revision; `evaluation/multiple_comparisons.py` now writes Holm-adjusted values within two pre-defined families per task (`results/paired_tests_holm.csv`), and the chapters quote both.
